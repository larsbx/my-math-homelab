### B.1 The import that carries the whole G1-free claim

Theorem 5.38 rests entirely on Imported Theorem 5.37. The referee record
(102 rounds) did not examine its source hypotheses, but the dedicated
2026-09-21 literature gate subsequently checked all three points below against
arXiv:1107.3598 and the published version:

1. that Theorem 3.1 accepts a periodic patch `Q̄` that is **not** a legal
   patch of `Ω_σ` (the swap word `ab` may be an illegal factor);
2. that "densely eventually coincident" means coincidence on a dense set of
   `x`, not on a set of positive or full measure. If it is the latter, the
   proof still goes through: Lemma 5.36(b) gives `G(s)` of full measure. The
   manuscript should state the full-measure form so the import is robust to
   either reading;
3. that the one-dimensional specialization is exactly the argument by which
   their Theorem 3.2 is derived from 3.1, as claimed.

**Discharged 2026-09-21 — `Proceed`.** All three points were checked against the source
text (arXiv:1107.3598 and the published version) and settled in the
manuscript's favour: BSW define coincidence for patches "not necessarily
allowed for `Φ`", "densely" means a dense set of points, and Theorem 3.2 is
derived from 3.1 by exactly the periodic-patch argument the manuscript
reuses. Unimodularity is not assumed. See
`docs/bsw-import-literature-gate-2026-09-21.md`.

## C. The overlap route against the reduction chain

This is the question the audit was asked. Notation: `C1` = SCC Producer,
`C2 ⟸ C3-local ⟸ C4` the boundary chain, `OP_seed` = all overlaps reachable from one selected swap seed productive,
and `OP_all` = manuscript Open Problem 5.35, every vertex of `O_σ` productive.

### C.1 Logical relationship (all proved in the manuscript)

```text
OP_all  ⟹  every reachable BPA state productive  ⟹  C1        (Thm 5.32 iv)
OP_seed ⟹  PDS                                               (Thm 5.38)
under G1:   OP_all  ⟺  C1                                    (Thm 5.32 iii,v + Prop 4.23)
under G1:   C4 ⟹ C3-local ⟹ C2 ⟹ C1                          (Prop 5.7 chain; one-way)
G1 ∧ C1 ⟹ PDS                                                (Thm 3.1)
```

`OP_all` strengthens `C1` through the displayed implication. No direct
implication between `OP_seed` and `C1` is established; `OP_seed` instead feeds
Theorem 5.38 directly and lives on a finite seed-relative graph. The C4 chain
is a sufficiency route for `C1` under G1 only. It cannot feed `OP_seed` directly: a nonproductive
overlap may lie inside a productive BPA state (Thm 5.32(ii) says a state is
productive iff *some* overlap of it is), so state-level productivity does
not imply overlap-level productivity without finiteness.

### C.3 What the new gate actually is

Three facts fix the difficulty of `OP_all`; they must not be attributed to
`OP_seed`:

1. `OP_all ⟹` two-sided strong coincidence (Prop 5.39(iii)). Strong coincidence
   is the Arnoux–Ito condition, a theorem for `d=2` (Barge–Diamond 2002) and
   open for `d ≥ 3`. So `OP` is at least as hard as a named open problem that
   predates this program by twenty-five years.
2. Under strong coincidence, `OP_all` reduces to the hitting problem
   `∃m: M^m w ∈ P_m(i) − P_m(j)` for every vertex (Cor 5.41(ii)). The
   manuscript identifies this (l.868) as "the geometric form of the
   coincidence problem studied in [IR, BK, MT]", i.e. super-coincidence /
   geometric coincidence. That is the second half of the classical
   decomposition PDS ⟺ strong coincidence + super-coincidence.
3. For unimodular `σ`, PDS ⟹ productivity of every vertex of `O_σ` can be
   read off Barge–Kwapisz Cor 9.4 / Prop 17.2 (manuscript l.793; not recorded
   as imported). With Thm 5.38 this makes `OP_all ⟺ PDS` in the unimodular
   case.

Consequently the program has, by its own route, arrived at the standard
equivalent form of the Pisot conjecture. That is a correctness signal for the
route and a novelty warning for the claim. "One open premise" is literally
true and rhetorically misleading: the premise is the conjecture, minus a
finiteness hypothesis that the literature's overlap algorithm (Akiyama–Lee,
via the Meyer property) also never needed. The genuine gains are narrower and
should be stated as such:

- Thm 4.22 gives finiteness of the *seed-patch* (possibly illegal) overlap
  graph from bounded discrepancy alone, with an explicit bound;
- Prop 5.43–5.44 and Lemma 5.45 give a normal form (Perron-critical, full
  rank, aligned-or-zipper, purely periodic cycle offsets) that is sharper
  than "residual component of full growth";
- Prop 5.42 is the first place the program uses Pisot contraction at the
  offset level rather than through eigenvalue moduli, which the 09-10 audit
  asked for. It is, however, a *lower* bound on the hitting depth (early hits
  are impossible), which is the wrong direction for forcing a hit.

## D. Assessment

1. **The route is sound and the record is honest.** Every theorem on the
   09-14 route checks, the manuscript's status vocabulary is used correctly,
   the referee's 28 mathematical findings were all accepted and all reduced
   claims, and the ledger now encodes the chain as one-way dependencies. The
   09-10 record-drift finding has been fully remediated.

2. **The BSW import check is discharged.** The dedicated 2026-09-21
   literature gate checked all three source hypotheses in B.1 and recorded
   `Proceed`: patches need not be allowed, "densely" means a dense set of
   points, the one-dimensional specialization is the source's own argument,
   and unimodularity is not assumed.

3. **Do not identify the two open premises.** `OP_all` contains strong
   coincidence, is equivalent to PDS for unimodular `σ`, and under strong
   coincidence is the every-vertex super-coincidence hitting problem.
   `OP_seed` is the weaker sufficient hypothesis used by Theorem 5.38; no
   implication from one selected seed to all vertices is established. The program has reconstructed the
   standard picture, which is reassuring about the mathematics and should
   temper the "one open premise" framing on every status surface.

4. **Pisot geometry still enters only through the spectrum, plus one lower
   bound.** The zipper branch's constraints (Perron-criticality, full rank,
   cycle equation, no vanishing tail) are all consequences of `spec M`. What
   a hit-forcing argument needs is an *upper* bound or a recurrence statement
   in the contracting space: the offsets of `S` are finitely many purely
   periodic `β`-expansions in the digit set `F` (Lemma 5.45), and the hitting
   condition asks whether the contracting projection of the prefix set of
   `σ^m(i)` minus that of `σ^m(j)` (which accumulates on Rauzy-fractal
   pieces) meets the projected offset. That is the (F)-property /
   super-coincidence geometry of Ito–Rao and, for `|det M|>1`,
   Minervino–Thuswaldner. None of the eleven notes engages it; the literature
   gate of 09-15 named it and set it aside. The firewall against a Euclidean
   internal space in the non-unimodular case is right, and it is also why the
   zipper branch cannot be closed by more bookkeeping on `Z^d`.

5. **The aligned branch is a cleaner, separately meaningful target.** Strong
   coincidence for ternary PIP substitutions is a named open problem. The BD
   eliminator, the hub-star normal form, and the coaccessible-core bound are
   genuinely about it and do not depend on the zipper machinery. Progress
   there is publishable on its own even if `OP` is not closed.

6. **Two dormant tracks are correctly parked.** G1b-2 (equivalent to G1) and
   the word-level sieves of programme B do not feed the headline. The
   manuscript's own discussion (l.1026) locates G1b-2 in an internal space
   with a non-Archimedean factor; nobody is working there.
