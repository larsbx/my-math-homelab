## 1. The statement

From Theorem C, the surviving aligned obligation is:

> **A1′.** Let `sigma` be PIP on three letters, `{a,b}` an eventually
> coincident pair, `c` the hub, and `h = sigma_+` the first-letter map. If `h`
> fixes both letters of a bad edge `{x, c}` (`x ∈ {a,b}`), then `{x, c}` is
> eventually coincident.

Equivalently (Corollary C2 there): `sigma(x) = x X` and `sigma(c) = c Y`, so
`u = sigma^infinity(x)` and `w = sigma^infinity(c)` are both `sigma`-fixed, and
A1′ says the tilings of `[0, infinity)` by `u` and by `w`, anchored at the same
point, share a tile.

## 2. Proposition A: A1′ is the residual half of SC_all

`strong-coincidence-census-2026-10-04.md` §1 splits aligned pairs by mechanism:
a pair `{i,j}` is **merged** when `h^n(i) = h^n(j)` for some `n`, and
**residual** otherwise. The aligned route of #138 concerns residual pairs only.

*Proposition A.* Under A1′'s hypothesis the bad edge `{x, c}` is residual, and
every eventual-coincidence witness for it is strictly interior:
`sigma^n(x) = p d s`, `sigma^n(c) = p' d s'` with `pi(p) = pi(p') != 0`.

*Proof.* `h(x) = x` and `h(c) = c` give `h^n(x) = x` and `h^n(c) = c` for every
`n`, and `x != c`, so `h^n(x) != h^n(c)` always: the pair is never merged.
A witness with `p = p' = empty` would need `sigma^n(x)` and `sigma^n(c)` to
begin with the same letter, i.e. `h^n(x) = h^n(c)`. So `p` is nonempty, and
`pi(p) = pi(p') != 0` because a nonempty word has nonzero Parikh vector.
`square`

*Consequence (exact, inherited).* The census decides every aligned pair
exactly with `psc.coincidence_formula`, which is finite by the Pisot property,
so a negative would be a verdict rather than an exhausted budget. It reports
**SC_all failing on 0 specimens** over four domains — the standing corpus
(4,554), total image length at most 8 (24,486), images of length at most 4
(135,990) and total length at most 10 (408,798) — with residual coincidence
levels at most 15, and at most 8 inside the catch-up-free class. A1′ is a
restriction of SC_all to one configuration, so **it holds on every one of those
408,798 specimens**. The deepest residual pair is level 15 on
`0 -> 1, 1 -> 2, 2 -> 10`, and the levels 12–15 tail has the same 30 pairs on
every domain: enlarging the domain adds no deep pairs.

*No sharper finite test exists.* A1′'s hypothesis presupposes a **bad** edge,
one that is not eventually coincident. No verified specimen has one, because
SC_all holds on all four domains. So there is nothing to census beyond SC_all
itself: any specimen satisfying A1′'s hypothesis non-vacuously would already
refute SC_all, hence PSC. That is why this note adds no new driver.

## 3. Corollary C3: on the catch-up-free class, A1′ is T2

*Lemma A* of the census (§3, proved there) says: for `sigma` catch-up-free with
`|det M| = 2`, if the aligned pair `(i, j, 0)` has an offset-zero child other
than its leftmost child `(h(i), h(j), 0)`, then `h(i) = h(j)`.

*Corollary C3.* Let `sigma` be PIP on three letters, catch-up-free with
`|det M| = 2`, and suppose A1′'s hypothesis holds for the bad edge `{x, c}`.
Then the aligned vertex `(x, c, 0)` is its own leftmost child and has **no
other offset-zero child**. Hence `{x, c}` can become eventually coincident only
through a nonzero-offset descendant that returns to offset zero, and by Lemma P
every such return is a simultaneous birth.

*Proof.* `h(x) = x` and `h(c) = c` make the leftmost child
`(h(x), h(c), 0) = (x, c, 0)` itself. Since `x != c` we have `h(x) != h(c)`, so
Lemma A's contrapositive forbids any other offset-zero child. A coincidence is
an offset-zero vertex with equal letters, so it is not `(x, c, 0)` and is not
an offset-zero child of it; any path to one therefore leaves offset zero and
returns. Lemma P (`p1b-vertex-coincidence-box-2026-10-02.md` §5.6b) says that
in the catch-up-free class no edge is a catch-up hit, so every return to offset
zero is a simultaneous birth. `square`

*Why this matters.* T2 of the box note's §5.6c is exactly the statement that in
the catch-up-free class every recurrent vertex has a descendant with an
offset-zero child through two positive indices — a simultaneous birth. So on
this class **the aligned branch of #138 and the strict-zipper branch of #139
are not two problems but one**: both reduce to boundary hitting by simultaneous
birth. The census already observed this for SC_all in general terms; what
Theorem C adds is that the *surviving* template is precisely the configuration
in which Lemma A's contrapositive applies, so the identification is forced
rather than incidental.

The class is not small: all 210 catch-up-free members of the standing corpus
have `|det M| = 2`, so Corollary C3 covers the whole catch-up-free class there;
at total image length at most 8, 570 of the 654 catch-up-free specimens do.

## 3a. Proposition D: the class forces an explicit normal form

Corollary C3 puts A1′ and T2 together on the catch-up-free `|det M| = 2`
class. On that class the surviving template can be written down completely.
Throughout, `O` is the odd letter set of Proposition C
(`p1b-vertex-coincidence-box-2026-10-02.md` §5.6b): `f(v) = sum_{c in O} v_c`
mod 2 cuts out `Λ = M Z^3`, every image column has `f = 0`, and every proper
nonempty prefix of an image has `f = 1`. Write `y` for a letter outside `O`.

*Lemma D0.* `|O| <= 2`.

*Proof.* Suppose `O` is all of `A`, so `f(v) = |v|` mod 2. Every column has
`f = 0`, so every image has even length; every proper nonempty prefix has
`f = 1`, so every proper nonempty prefix has odd length. If some
`|sigma(a)| >= 3` then its length-2 prefix is proper and even, a
contradiction. So every image has length at most two, and being even, exactly
two. Then every column of `M` sums to `2`, so `1^T M = 2 · 1^T`; as `M` is
primitive with the positive left vector `1`, its Perron root is `beta = 2`.
A rational Perron root contradicts irreducibility of the characteristic
polynomial. `square`

*Proposition D (normal form).* Let `sigma` be PIP on three letters,
catch-up-free with `|det M| = 2`, and suppose the aligned branch's template of
Theorem C holds in **case (i)**: `h(x) = x` and `h(c) = c` for a bad edge
`{x, c}`. Then `O = {x, c}` exactly, the third letter `y` is the only even
letter, and

    sigma(x) = x y^p s_x,    sigma(c) = c y^q s_c,    sigma(y) = t y^r s_y

with `p, q, r >= 0` and `s_x, s_c, t, s_y ∈ {x, c}`.

*Proof.* If `|sigma(x)| = 1` then `sigma(x)` is its own first letter, which is
`h(x) = x`, so `sigma(x) = x` and `M e_x = e_x`; then `M^n e_x = e_x` for all
`n`, while primitivity makes `M^n` strictly positive for large `n`, so
`M^n e_x` has three positive coordinates and `e_x` does not. So
`|sigma(x)| >= 2`, and by Proposition C its first letter `x` is in `O`. The
same argument gives `|sigma(c)| >= 2` and `c ∈ O`. With `x != c` this gives
`|O| >= 2`, so `|O| = 2` by Lemma D0 and `O = {x, c}`. The only letter outside
`O` is `y`, so Proposition C's shape — an `O`-letter, then letters outside `O`,
then an `O`-letter — is exactly the display above, and `|sigma(y)| >= 2` by
the same primitivity argument applied to `sigma(y) = y`. `square`

*Corollary D1 (the one-odd-letter sub-class is clear).* On the 18 catch-up-free
corpus specimens with `|O| = 1` (census §5.6b), case (i) of the template cannot
occur: it would need two distinct odd letters.

*Corollary D2 (the other two templates force a length-one image).* On the same
class, in the **alternating-E** template `sigma(a) = b` or `sigma(b) = a`, a
single letter, and that letter is even; and in **case (ii)** either `O = {x,c}`
as above, or one of `sigma(x)`, `sigma(c)` is a single letter.

*Proof.* Alternating: `h(c) = c`, `h(a) = b`, `h(b) = a`. If all three images
had length at least two, Proposition C would put each image's first letter in
`O`, giving `c, b, a ∈ O` and `|O| = 3`, which Lemma D0 forbids. So some image
has length one, and a length-one image is a single letter outside `O`.
`sigma(c) = c` is excluded by the primitivity argument above, so the
length-one image is `sigma(a) = b` or `sigma(b) = a`. Case (ii) is the same
argument with `h(x) = c`, `h(c) = x`: if both images have length at least two
their first letters `c` and `x` are odd and `O = {x, c}`; and `sigma(x) = c`
with `sigma(c) = x` together give `M^2 e_x = e_x`, again excluded, so at most
one is a single letter. `square`

So on the catch-up-free `|det M| = 2` class every surviving aligned template
either has the explicit three-parameter normal form of Proposition D, or has a
length-one image — and the second branch lands in the sub-class already studied
in `p1b-vertex-coincidence-box-2026-10-02.md` §5.6g, one E letter with a
length-1 image, where Lemma B there proves that powers never supply a Barge-class
conjugacy.

## 3b. Theorem E: A1′ holds on the whole catch-up-free determinant-2 class

The sweep of §3a was evidence. This section turns it into a proof.

*Theorem E.* Let `sigma` be PIP on three letters, catch-up-free, with
`|det M| = 2`. If the first-letter map `h` fixes two distinct letters `x` and
`c`, then the pair `{x, c}` is eventually coincident.

*Corollary E1.* On the catch-up-free `|det M| = 2` class, **case (i) of the
aligned template cannot occur**: it requires a pair `{x, c}` that `h` fixes
pointwise and that is *not* eventually coincident, which Theorem E forbids.

This is a strong-coincidence statement on an infinite class, not on a finite
list. Every member of the class, of every image length, is covered. The class
contains all 210 catch-up-free specimens of the standing corpus. §3b.8 records
what the theorem does and does not cover in the programme.

The proof has seven steps. Steps 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6 are elementary; step 4 is
three explicit witness computations; step 7 is a finite exact check of 27
substitutions.

### 3b.1 Reduction to the normal form

Proposition D needs exactly the hypotheses of Theorem E — catch-up-free,
`|det M| = 2`, and `h(x) = x`, `h(c) = c` with `x != c` — and nothing about
templates or eventual coincidence. So `sigma` is, up to naming the letters,

    sigma(x) = x y^p s_x,   sigma(c) = c y^q s_c,   sigma(y) = t y^r s_y,

with `p, q, r >= 0` and the four endings in `{x, c}`. Conversely every PIP
member of this family is catch-up-free, and its first-letter map fixes `x`
and `c`. Put `f(v) = v_x + v_c` mod 2. Each image has exactly two letters from
`{x, c}`, so every column of `M` has `f = 0` and `Λ = M Z^3 <= ker f`. Every
proper nonempty prefix of an image is one letter from `{x, c}` followed by
`y`s, so it has `f = 1` and is not in `Λ`, which is Lemma P's condition.
**So Theorem E is exactly the statement that every PIP member of this family
with `|det M| = 2` has `{x, c}` eventually coincident**, and nothing outside
the family is in question.

### 3b.2 The determinant is affine, and pins one parameter

The parameters `p, q, r` occur only in the bottom row of `M` (the `y`-counts of
the three images), so `det M` is affine in `(p, q, r)`. Evaluating it exactly:

| `(s_x, s_c, t, s_y)` | `det M` | | `(s_x, s_c, t, s_y)` | `det M` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `(x,x,x,x)` | `2(r − p)` | | `(c,x,x,x)` | `2(q − p)` |
| `(x,x,x,c)` | `2(r − q)` | | `(c,x,x,c)` | `0` |
| `(x,x,c,x)` | `2(r − q)` | | `(c,x,c,x)` | `0` |
| `(x,x,c,c)` | `2(p − 2q + r)` | | `(c,x,c,c)` | `2(p − q)` |
| `(x,c,x,x)` | `4(r − p)` | | `(c,c,x,x)` | `2(q + r − 2p)` |
| `(x,c,x,c)` | `2(2r − p − q)` | | `(c,c,x,c)` | `2(r − p)` |
| `(x,c,c,x)` | `2(2r − p − q)` | | `(c,c,c,x)` | `2(r − p)` |
| `(x,c,c,c)` | `4(r − q)` | | `(c,c,c,c)` | `2(r − q)` |

So `|det M| = 2` is a **linear Diophantine condition** — `|p − q| = 1`,
`|r − q| = 1` or `|p − 2q + r| = 1` for the endings that survive — and each
surviving family is two-dimensional. This is why the sweep never met `p = q`
in classes where the condition is `|p − q| = 1`.

### 3b.3 Eight endings have no PIP member

- **Not primitive** (four): in `(x,x,x,x)` and `(x,c,x,x)` the letter `c`
  occurs in no image but `sigma(c)`; in `(x,c,c,c)` and `(c,c,c,c)` the letter
  `x` occurs in no image but `sigma(x)`. Either way a power of `M` keeps a zero
  row. Two of them fail `|det M| = 2` first: `(x,c,x,x)` and `(x,c,c,c)`
  have `det M = 4(r − p)` and `4(r − q)`, a multiple of 4.
- **Singular** (two): `(c,x,x,c)` and `(c,x,c,x)` have `det M = 0`.
- **A rational eigenvalue** (two): `(x,c,x,c)` and `(x,c,c,x)` both have
  `M = [[2,0,1],[0,2,1],[p,q,r]]`, and `(1, −1, 0) M = (2, −2, 0)`, so `2` is
  an eigenvalue and the characteristic cubic has the rational root 2.

### 3b.4 The other eight are four classes, by mirror symmetry

Relabelling `x <-> c` sends the normal form to itself with
`(p, q) -> (q, p)`, `s_x -> swap(s_c)`, `s_c -> swap(s_x)`, `t -> swap(t)`,
`s_y -> swap(s_y)`. It is a conjugation of `M` by a permutation matrix, so it
preserves PIP and `|det M|`, and it maps the pair `{x, c}` to itself, so it
preserves eventual coincidence. The eight surviving endings pair off into

| class | ending | mirror | `sigma(y)` | `|det M| = 2` means |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A** | `(x,x,x,c)` | `(c,c,c,x)` | `x y^r c` | `|r − q| = 1` |
| **B** | `(x,x,c,x)` | `(c,c,x,c)` | `c y^r x` | `|r − q| = 1` |
| **C** | `(x,x,c,c)` | `(c,c,x,x)` | `c y^r c` | `|p − 2q + r| = 1` |
| **D** | `(c,x,c,c)` | `(c,x,x,x)` | `c y^r c` | `|p − q| = 1` |

with `sigma(x) = x y^p x`, `sigma(c) = c y^q x` in classes A–C and
`sigma(x) = x y^p c`, `sigma(c) = c y^q x` in class D. It suffices to prove
Theorem E for these four.

### 3b.5 Two Pisot necessary conditions, which are also affine

Write `f(t) = det(tI − M) = (t − beta)(t − beta_2)(t − beta_3)`.

*Lemma P1.* If `M` is PIP then `f(1) < 0` and `f(−1) < 0`.

*Proof.* `1 − beta < 0`. If `beta_2, beta_3` are real they lie in `(−1, 1)`,
so `(1 − beta_2)(1 − beta_3) > 0`; if they are complex conjugates the product
is `|1 − beta_2|^2 > 0`. So `f(1) < 0`. Likewise
`f(−1) = −(1 + beta)(1 + beta_2)(1 + beta_3)`, with `1 + beta > 0` and
`(1 + beta_2)(1 + beta_3) > 0` by the same two cases. `square`

Like the determinant, `f(±1)` is affine in `(p, q, r)`:

| class | `f(1)` | `f(−1)` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A, B | `q − p` | `p + 3q − 6r − 6` |
| C | `2(q − p)` | `−2p + 6q − 6r − 6` |
| D | `−2p + r − 1` | `−2p + 4q − 3r − 3` |

The first column already explains a pattern of the sweep: **in classes A, B
and C every PIP member has `p > q`.**

### 3b.6 Three witness lemmas

Each lemma names a position and checks that it is a shared tile: equal Parikh
prefixes — hence equal length, since a Parikh vector's components sum to the
length — followed by the same letter. A **position** `s` is the length of
that common prefix, so the shared tile is the letter at 0-based index `s` of
both words. Write `pre_w(j)` for the Parikh vector of the length-`j` prefix of
a word `w`; an *offset* `j` inside a block is used the same way.

*Lemma L_BC (classes B and C, level 2).* If `p > q`, `r >= 1` and `q >= 1`,
then `sigma^2(x)` and `sigma^2(c)` share a tile at position
`(p + 2) + q(r + 2) + 1`.

*Proof.* Here `t = c`. Write `sigma^2(x) = sigma(x) sigma(y)^p sigma(x)` and
`sigma^2(c) = sigma(c) sigma(y)^q sigma(x)`, and look at level-1 block number
`q + 1` in each. Since `q + 1 <= p` it is a `sigma(y)` block in `sigma^2(x)`;
in `sigma^2(c)` it is the final `sigma(x)`. The Parikh vectors before those
blocks differ by
`pi(sigma(x)) − pi(sigma(c)) = (2e_x + p e_y) − (e_c + q e_y + e_x) = e_x − e_c + (p − q) e_y`.
Take offset 1 in `sigma(y) = c y^r s_y`, which adds `e_c`, and offset
`p − q + 1` in `sigma(x) = x y^p x`, which adds `e_x + (p − q) e_y`. The two
totals agree. The letters there are `y` and `y`, since `r >= 1` and `q >= 1`.
`square`

(A clause `r = q = 0`, `s_y = x`, where the letters are `s_y` and `x`, would
also work, but it never applies: class B has `|r − q| = 1` and class C has
`s_y = c`.)

*Lemma L_D (class D, level 2).* If `|p − q| = 1`, `min(p, q) >= 1` and
`r >= 2`, then `sigma^2(x)` and `sigma^2(c)` share a `y`-tile, at position
`p + 3` if `p = q + 1` and `p + 4` if `p = q − 1`.

*Proof.* `sigma^2(x) = sigma(x) sigma(y)^p sigma(c)` and
`sigma^2(c) = sigma(c) sigma(y)^q sigma(x)`; with `min(p, q) >= 1`, block 1 is
a `sigma(y) = c y^r c` in both. Before it the Parikh vectors differ by
`pi(sigma(x)) − pi(sigma(c)) = (p − q) e_y = ±e_y`. Inside
`c y^r c`, `pre(j) = e_c + (j − 1) e_y` for `1 <= j <= r + 1`, so offsets that
differ by one absorb the `±e_y`; the letters at offsets 1 and 2 are both `y`
exactly when `r >= 2`. `square`

*Lemma L_A (class A, level 3).* Let `p > q` and `|r − q| = 1`. Then
`sigma^3(x)` and `sigma^3(c)` share a `y`-tile, at position
`p + q + 6 + q(r + 2)` if `r = q + 1` and `p + q + 5 + q(r + 2)` if
`r = q − 1` — except at `(1, 0, 1)`, on the line `(p, 1, 0)` with `p >= 3`, and
on the line `(p, 2, 1)` with `p >= 4`.

*Proof.* Class A has `t = x`, so Lemma L_BC's cancellation is not available
and level 2 has no witness; the cancellation happens one level up. Take the
level-2 prefixes `P = x y^{q+1}` of `sigma^2(x)`, inside its first block
`x y^p x` because `q + 1 <= p`, and `Q = sigma(c) = c y^q x` of `sigma^2(c)`.
Both have length `q + 2`. `P` is followed by `y` if `p >= q + 2` and by `x`
if `p = q + 1`. `Q` is followed by `x`, because the next block is a
`sigma(y) = x y^r c` when `q >= 1` and the final `sigma(x)` when `q = 0`.
Then

    M(pi(P) − pi(Q)) = M(e_y − e_c) = pi(sigma(y)) − pi(sigma(c)) = (r − q) e_y = ±e_y.

Inside the next level-1 blocks this `±e_y` is again absorbed by offsets that
differ by one. On the x-side the block is `sigma(y) = x y^r c` or
`sigma(x) = x y^p x`; on the c-side it is `sigma(x) = x y^p x`. Each has
`pre(j) = e_x + (j − 1) e_y` for `j >= 1`. The four sub-cases:

| | `p >= q + 2` (x-side `sigma(y)`) | `p = q + 1` (x-side `sigma(x)`) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `r = q + 1` | offsets 1, 2; letters `y`, `y` since `r >= 1`, `p >= 2` — **always** | offsets 1, 2; needs `p >= 2`, so fails only at `q = 0`: **(1,0,1)** |
| `r = q − 1` | offsets 2, 1; needs `r >= 2`, i.e. `q >= 3`: **fails for `q ∈ {1, 2}`** | offsets 2, 1; needs `p >= 2`, which holds since `q = r + 1 >= 1` — **always** |

`square`

## 3c. From A1′ to all-pairs strong coincidence on the `|O| = 2` class

Theorem E is about one pair. One elementary lemma turns it into a statement
about **every** pair, on its own class and on most of the surrounding one. Write
`H({i, j}) = {h(i), h(j)}` for the pair map induced by the first-letter map.

*Lemma T (prefix transfer).* For letters `i, j` and `n >= 0`, if `{h(i), h(j)}`
has a shared tile at level `n`, then `{i, j}` has one at level `n + 1`, at the
same position. If `h(i) = h(j)`, then `{i, j}` has a shared tile at level 1,
position 0.

*Proof.* `sigma^(n+1)(i) = sigma^n(sigma(i))` begins with `sigma^n(h(i))`, and
likewise for `j`. A shared tile at position `s` reads only the length-`s`
prefixes and the letter at `s`, so a shared tile of the two shorter words is a
shared tile of the two longer ones. For `h(i) = h(j)`, `sigma(i)` and
`sigma(j)` begin with the same letter after the empty prefix. `square`

*Corollary T1.* The `H`-orbit of a pair of distinct letters either reaches a
single letter, and then the pair is eventually coincident, or enters a cycle of
pairs of distinct letters. **All-pairs strong coincidence holds if and only if
every such `H`-cycle contains an eventually coincident pair.** For the
nontrivial direction, Lemma T carries a coincidence backwards along `H`: from
one pair of a cycle to its predecessor, so around the whole cycle, and then
back along any orbit that enters it.

*Corollary E2 (strong coincidence on Theorem E's class).* Every PIP
substitution `sigma(x) = x y^p s_x`, `sigma(c) = c y^q s_c`,
`sigma(y) = t y^r s_y` with `|det M| = 2` satisfies the strong coincidence
condition **for every pair of letters**.

*Proof.* `h(y) = t ∈ {x, c}`, so `H({x, y}) = {x, t}` and `H({c, y}) = {c, t}`
are each a single letter or `{x, c}`. Theorem E makes `{x, c}` eventually
coincident, and Lemma T does the rest. `square`

### 3c.1 The `|O| = 2` class, sorted by its `H`-cycles

Let `sigma` be PIP, catch-up-free, `|det M| = 2`, with odd letter set
`O = {x, c}` and even letter `y`. By Proposition C every image is either the
single letter `y`, or an odd letter, a run of `y`s, an odd letter. `sigma(y)`
is not `y` (primitivity), and `sigma(x)`, `sigma(c)` are not both `y` (two
equal columns). Up to the mirror `x <-> c`, that leaves:

| first-letter map | `H`-cycles of distinct pairs | closed by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| no image of length one, `h` fixes `x` and `c` | `{x, c}` fixed | Theorem E (Corollary E2) |
| no image of length one, `h(x) = h(c)` | none | Lemma T alone |
| no image of length one, `h` swaps `x` and `c` | `{x, c}` fixed | **open — the swap family, §3c.3** |
| `sigma(x) = y`, `h(c) = c`, `h(y) = c` | none | Lemma T alone |
| `sigma(x) = y`, `h(c) = x`, `h(y) = c` | the 3-cycle `{x,y} -> {c,y} -> {x,c} -> {x,y}` | Barge–Diamond: some pair coincides, and all three are on the cycle |
| `sigma(x) = y`, `h(c) = x`, `h(y) = x` | `{x, y}` fixed | Proposition Y, family F2 |
| `sigma(x) = y`, `h(c) = c`, `h(y) = x` | `{x, y}` fixed; `{x, c} <-> {c, y}` | Proposition Y, family F1 |

The Barge–Diamond row uses their Theorem 1 exactly as imported (§4): *some*
pair is strongly coincident. On a single cycle through all three pairs, any
one suffices.

*On the standing corpus* (`two_odd_survey` in
`kernel/swap_family_certificate.mojo`, pinned by
`test_the_corpus_two_odd_letter_specimens_fit_the_table`): all 210
catch-up-free specimens have `|det M| = 2` and a unique odd set; 18 have one

90 (`h(x) = h(c)`), 36 (swap), 12 (`y`-constant), 18 (3-cycle), 6 (F2) and
0 (F1, whose Pisot range `r < q < 3r` needs an image of length at least 4).
Every specimen fits a row; the survey raises on one that does not.

### 3c.2 Proposition Y: the two `sigma(x) = y` families

Write `sigma(c) = a y^q b`, `sigma(y) = d y^r e`. Each of the last two rows of
the table keeps exactly one ending. In the other six a row of `M` is zero or a
unit row, so `M` is not primitive, or the `x`- and `c`-rows are equal, so
`det M = 0`:

| family | `sigma` | `det M` | `f(1)` | `f(−1)` | Lemma P1 gives |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **F1** | `y`, `c y^q c`, `x y^r c` | `−2` | `r − q` | `q − 3r` | `r < q < 3r` |
| **F2** | `y`, `x y^q x`, `x y^r c` | `2` | `−q − r − 2` | `q − r − 2` | `q <= r + 1` |

So `|det M| = 2` costs nothing here: it holds identically.

*Lemma L_y (both families, level 2).* If `r >= 2`, then `sigma^2(x)` and
`sigma^2(y)` share a `y`-tile at position 2.

*Proof.* `sigma^2(x) = sigma(y) = x y^r c` begins `x y y`.
`sigma^2(y) = sigma(x) sigma(y)^r sigma(c) = y · x y^r c ···` begins `y x y`.
The length-2 prefixes both have Parikh vector `e_x + e_y`, and both letters at
position 2 are `y`. `square`

*Lemma L_cy (family F1, level 2).* If `q > r >= 1`, then `sigma^2(c)` and
`sigma^2(y)` share a `y`-tile at position `q + r^2 + r + 1`.

*Proof.* `sigma^2(c) = sigma(c) sigma(y)^q sigma(c)` with `|sigma(c)| = q + 2`
and `|sigma(y)| = r + 2`. The position is `(q + 2) + (r − 1)(r + 2) + 1`:
offset 1 of the `r`-th `sigma(y)` block, which exists because `r <= q`, and
whose letter there is `y` because `r >= 1`. The prefix has Parikh vector
`(2e_c + q e_y) + (r − 1)(e_x + r e_y + e_c) + e_x = r e_x + (r + 1) e_c + (q + r^2 − r) e_y`.
`sigma^2(y) = sigma(x) sigma(y)^r sigma(c)`, and `sigma(x) sigma(y)^r` has
length `1 + r(r + 2)`, so the position is offset `q − r` of the final
`sigma(c) = c y^q c`, which lies in its `y`-run because `1 <= q − r <= q`. The
prefix has Parikh vector
`e_y + r(e_x + r e_y + e_c) + e_c + (q − r − 1) e_y = r e_x + (r + 1) e_c + (q + r^2 − r) e_y`.
The two agree. `square`

*Proposition Y.* Every PIP member of families F1 and F2 satisfies the strong
coincidence condition for every pair.

*Proof.* **F1.** Lemma P1 gives `q > r >= 1`, so Lemma L_cy makes `{c, y}`
coincident, and Lemma T gives `{x, c}`, whose `H`-image is `{y, c}`. For
`{x, y}`, Lemma L_y covers `r >= 2`; at `r = 1` Lemma P1 leaves only
`(q, r) = (2, 1)`, which the exact screen confirms PIP and
`coincidence_level` decides coincident at level 4. **F2.** Lemma L_y covers
`r >= 2`. At `r <= 1` Lemma P1 leaves `(0,0)`, `(1,0)`, `(0,1)`, `(1,1)`,
`(2,1)`; the exact screen finds only `(1,1)` and `(2,1)` PIP, and both are
coincident at level 3. The other pairs follow by Lemma T, since
`H({x, c}) = {y, x}` and `H({c, y}) = {x}`. `square`

### 3c.3 What is left: the swap family

*Theorem G (reduction).* On the catch-up-free `|det M| = 2` class with
`|O| = 2`, every PIP substitution satisfies the strong coincidence condition
for every pair, **except possibly** the members of the swap family

    sigma(x) = c y^p s_x,   sigma(c) = x y^q s_c,   sigma(y) = t y^r s_y,

and for those it is equivalent to the single pair `{x, c}` being eventually
coincident. *Proof:* §3c.1's table, with Corollary E2, Lemma T, Barge–Diamond
and Proposition Y. `square`

The swap family is Theorem E's problem over again, without its first-letter
fixed points: `{x, c}` is its own `H`-image, so no transfer reaches it. Six of
its sixteen endings carry no PIP member — `(x,c,x,c)` and `(x,c,c,x)` have
`det M = 0`; `(c,x,x,x)` and `(c,x,c,c)` have `det M ∈ 4Z`; `(c,x,x,c)` and
`(c,x,c,x)` have the left eigenvector `(1, −1, 0)` for the rational eigenvalue
`−2` — and the other ten form five mirror pairs. One of them,
`(x, c, x, x)`, is the reversal of Theorem E's class D, with the same
incidence matrices and the same Lemma L_D mechanism, but different levels:
reversal preserves `M`, not coincidence.

*Exact sweep (exploratory, stated budget).* `swap_census` in the same driver
decides `{x, c}` with `coincidence_level` for every PIP member with
`|det M| = 2` and `p, q, r <= 6` and `<= 9`: **328 and 724 members, every one
eventually coincident**, deepest level 7, at `(0, 1, 0)` with ending
`(x, c, x, x)` and its mirror. The rows at levels 5–7 (8, 6, 2) are identical
at both bounds; level 4 grows (38 to 54), because it contains infinite lines —
`(n + 1, 0, n)` in ending `(x, x, x, c)` and `(0, n + 1, n)` in `(x, x, x, x)`.
So a Theorem E-style proof here needs witness lemmas at level 4, not only 2
and 3. The sweep is evidence only; **§3d is the proof** (Theorem H).

*What §3c establishes, and what it does not.* It proves all-pairs strong
coincidence on Theorem E's class and on the two `sigma(x) = y` families, and it
reduces the whole catch-up-free `|det M| = 2`, `|O| = 2` class to one pair in
one explicit family, which §3d then settles. It says nothing about the 18 corpus specimens with
`|O| = 1` (Corollary D1), whose image shapes are different, nor outside
determinant 2. Strong coincidence on a class does not by itself give pure
discrete spectrum there; it is one hypothesis of the routes the programme
tracks, and no ledger node changes.

### 3d.1 Witness paths

*Lemma W.* Let `sigma` be a substitution with incidence matrix `M`. Take letters
`a_0, b_0` and, for `l = 0, ..., n − 1`, positions `i_l < |sigma(a_l)|`,
`k_l < |sigma(b_l)|`, with `a_(l+1) = sigma(a_l)[i_l]`,
`b_(l+1) = sigma(b_l)[k_l]`, `gamma_0 = 0` and

    gamma_(l+1) = M gamma_l + pi(sigma(a_l)[:i_l]) − pi(sigma(b_l)[:k_l]).

If `gamma_n = 0` and `a_n = b_n`, then `sigma^n(a_0)` and `sigma^n(b_0)` share
a tile.

*Proof.* Put `s_0 = 0` and `s_(l+1) = |sigma(sigma^l(a_0)[:s_l])| + i_l`. By
induction `sigma^l(a_0)[s_l] = a_l` and
`pi(sigma^(l+1)(a_0)[:s_(l+1)]) = M pi(sigma^l(a_0)[:s_l]) + pi(sigma(a_l)[:i_l])`;
likewise `t_l` on the `b` side. Subtracting, the difference of the two prefix
Parikh vectors at level `l` is `gamma_l`. At level `n` it is 0, so the prefixes
have equal Parikh vectors, hence equal lengths, and the letters there are
`a_n = b_n`. `square`

*The cone version.* Let `(p, q, r) = base + sum_k n_k g_k` with `n_k >= 0`, so
every run length, and every entry of `M`, is an affine form in the `n_k`.
Describe each position as a segment of the image — a single letter or the `y`-run —
plus an affine offset inside it. If every `gamma_l` is a **constant** vector,
then `M gamma_l` is affine, and each condition of Lemma W becomes either an
affine identity (the new `gamma` is the constant the path says) or an affine
inequality (an offset `o` in a run of length `L` satisfies `0 <= o <= L − 1`).
An affine form whose constant and coefficients are all nonnegative is
nonnegative on all of `Z_{>=0}^m`. So **one check of the path proves Lemma W's
shared tile for every member of the cone at once.** `psc.cone_witness`
implements this: `verify_witness` re-derives every state from the step data
and checks every identity and inequality; `search_witness` finds candidate
paths by breadth-first search over the finitely many states `(a, b, gamma)`
with `|gamma_i| <= 3` and at most five steps. The search only proposes; a path
counts only once it verifies.

### 3d.2 The planes, and why each lies in a quadrant

Six endings carry no PIP member (§3c.3). For the other ten, `det M`, `f(1)` and
`f(−1)` are affine in `(p, q, r)` — the parameters sit only in the `y`-row of
`M` — and on the five classes they are:

| class | ending | `det M` | `f(1)` | `f(−1)` | quadrant: `(p, q, r) =` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| S1 | `(x,x,x,x)` | `2(q − r)` | `−2p − 2q + 2r − 2` | `2(p − q)` | `(u, u + 1 + v, u + 1 + v ± 1)` |
| S2 | `(x,x,x,c)` | `2(p − r)` | `−3p − q + 2r − 2` | `q − p` | `(u + 1 + v, u, u + 1 + v ± 1)` |
| S3 | `(x,x,c,x)` | `2(p − r)` | as S2 | as S2 | as S2 |
| S4 | `(x,x,c,c)` | `2(2p − q − r)` | `−4p + 2r − 2` | `4(q − p)` | `(u + 1 + v, u, u + 2 + 2v ± 1)` |
| S5 | `(x,c,x,x)` | `2(q − p)` | `−2q + r − 1` | `4p − 2q − 3r − 3` | `(u, u + 1, v)` and `(u + 1, u, v)` |

with `u, v >= 0`, one quadrant for each sign — so twenty quadrants in all with
the mirrors. *Why every PIP point is in one.* `|det M| = 2` is the displayed
pair of planes. In S1, Lemma P1's `f(−1) < 0` is `p < q`, so `q = p + 1 + v`;
in S2, S3 and S4 it is `q < p`, so `p = q + 1 + v`; in S5 the plane is already
`q = p ± 1`. Substituting gives the quadrant column, and nonnegativity of
`p, q, r` is automatic there (S1's minus branch has `r = u + v`). The mirror
`x <-> c` maps each class to its mirror ending with `(p, q) -> (q, p)`; the
certificate runs the mirrored quadrants directly rather than appealing to
symmetry.

### 3d.3 The cover

Each quadrant is split by a fixed recursion. A region — a quadrant, a line or a
point — is first offered to the witness-path search as a cone; failing that,
it is **cut** if `f(1)` or `f(−1)` is a nonnegative affine form on it, so that
Lemma P1 leaves no PIP point there; failing that, a quadrant
`{u >= u_0, v >= v_0}` splits as the line `u = u_0`, the line `v = v_0` with
`u > u_0`, and the quadrant `{u >= u_0 + 1, v >= v_0 + 1}`, and a line splits
as its first point and the rest. These are partitions, so the leaves cover
the quadrant exactly. A single point is screened exactly and, if PIP, decided
by `coincidence_level`. At depth 8 an unresolved region would be reported as a
failure, never as covered; none occurs.

| class | certified cones (witness levels) | cuts | points: PIP, at level |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| S1 | 4 (3; 3, 3, 4) | 0 | `(0,1,0)` 4, `(0,2,1)` 4 |
| S2 | 6 (3, 3, 3; 4, 4, 4) | 0 | `(1,0,2)` 5, `(1,0,0)` 6, `(2,0,1)` 5 |
| S3 | 6 (3, 3, 3; 4, 4, 3) | 0 | `(1,0,2)` 4, `(1,0,0)` 5 |
| S4 | 2 (4; 4) | 0 | none |
| S5 | 3 (5, 3; 3) | 3 | `(0,1,0)` 7, `(0,1,1)` 6, `(0,1,2)` 6; and `(1,0,0)`, not PIP |

and the same again for the mirrors at `(q, p, r)`: **42 cones, 6 cuts, and 20
PIP points, all eventually coincident.** S5's cuts are the lines
`(0, 1, 3 + n)` (`f(1) = n`), `(1, 0, 1 + n)` (`f(1) = n`) and
`(n + 2, n + 1, 0)` (`f(−1) = 2n + 3`). Every bulk quadrant is a single
cone; the level-4 infinite lines of the sweep (§3c.3) are cones too, with
uniform level-4 paths.

That proves Theorem H. `square`

### 3d.5 What Theorem H and Corollary H1 do and do not cover

They cover every substitution of the two-odd-letter catch-up-free
`|det M| = 2` class, of every image length — an infinite class — and give
the strong coincidence condition for **all three pairs** there. They do not
cover the one-odd-letter sub-class, whose images `o w o` carry arbitrary words
`w` over two letters rather than one run, so the parameters are not finitely
many; nor anything outside determinant 2. Strong coincidence is a *necessary*
condition for pure discrete spectrum here
([`pds-strong-coincidence-literature-gate-2026-10-04.md`](pds-strong-coincidence-literature-gate-2026-10-04.md),
Akiyama–Lee Corollary 4.5), so Corollary H1 discharges that necessary
condition on its class, and in particular the aligned obligation of #138
there. It is not sufficient as imported: Akiyama's equivalence with overlap
coincidence needs many choices of control points (gate note §2), and
Nakaishi's claimed converse is unimodular and unimported. So nothing about
the spectrum, G1 or PSC follows here without a further argument. The method is
the novel ingredient on our side; the idea of a finite certificate for an
infinite family is Berthé–Bourdon–Jolivet–Siegel's, in a different (unimodular,
product) setting. Theorem H has had no review beyond its author.

## 3e. The one-odd-letter class, and what is left of the whole class

With one odd letter `o`, Proposition C's shape is: every image is a single
letter other than `o`, or `o w o` with `w` any word over the two other letters
`y, z`. So `h(a) = o` for every image of length at least two.

*Proposition O.* Let `sigma` be PIP, catch-up-free, `|det M| = 2`, with one odd
letter `o`. Then `sigma` satisfies the strong coincidence condition for every
pair, **unless `sigma(o)` is a single letter** `y`; in that case it is
equivalent to `{o, y}` being eventually coincident.

*Proof.* Count the images of length one.

- *None.* `h` is constant, so every pair has a single-letter `H`-image
  (Lemma T).
- *One, `sigma(a) = b` with `a != o`.* Say `a = y`; then `b = z` (`b != o`
  because it is a short image, `b != y` by primitivity). `h(y) = z` and
  `h(o) = h(z) = o`, so `H({o, z}) = {o}`, `H({o, y}) = {o, z}`,
  `H({y, z}) = {z, o}`: every orbit reaches a single letter.
- *One, `sigma(o) = y`.* `h(y) = h(z) = o`, so `H({y, z}) = {o}` and
  `H({o, z}) = {y, o}`, while `H({o, y}) = {y, o}` is fixed. Corollary T1
  leaves exactly the pair `{o, y}`.
- *Two.* The long image is then the only one containing `o`, so the `o`-row of
  `M` has one nonzero entry. If the long letter is `o`, that row is
  `(2, 0, 0)` and `2` is an eigenvalue, against irreducibility. Otherwise, up
  to naming, `sigma(y) = o w o`, `sigma(z) = y` (not `z`, not `o`) and
  `sigma(o) = z` (`sigma(o) = y` would repeat a column, `det M = 0`). Then
  `h` cycles `o -> z -> y -> o`, `H` cycles all three pairs, and Barge–Diamond
  closes the cycle as in §3c.1.
- *Three* is impossible: `o` would occur in no image. `square`

*On the standing corpus* (`two_odd_survey`, same test): the 18 one-odd-letter
specimens are 6 with no image of length one and 12 with a short `sigma(a)`,
`a != o`. **None lies in the open row.**

*Theorem K (the catch-up-free determinant-2 class).* Every PIP, catch-up-free
substitution on three letters with `|det M| = 2` satisfies the strong
coincidence condition for every pair of letters, except possibly the members
of the family

    sigma(o) = y,   sigma(y) = o w_1 o,   sigma(z) = o w_2 o,   w_1, w_2 ∈ {y, z}*,

where it is equivalent to `{o, y}` being eventually coincident. *Proof:*
Lemma D0 (`|O| <= 2`), Corollary H1 for `|O| = 2`, Proposition O for
`|O| = 1`. `square`

*Corollary K1.* **All 210 catch-up-free specimens of the standing corpus
satisfy the strong coincidence condition for every pair, by proof** — every
one of them is outside the exceptional family. The strong-coincidence census
had already observed this; Theorem K is the first reason for it that does not
enumerate.

*What is left.* The exceptional family is not a finite-parameter family:
`w_1`, `w_2` are arbitrary words, so §3d's cones do not apply as they stand;
each fixed *pattern* of runs in `w_1, w_2` is a finite-parameter family of the
same kind as the swap family, but there are infinitely many patterns. It is
also where the swap family's `H`-fixed pair reappears, now between an odd and
an even letter. Catch-up-free substitutions of other determinant exist (84 of
the 654 at total length at most 8, §3), and none of this applies to them:
Proposition C's parity description needs `|det M| = 2`.

## 3f. Theorem K's open family: two lemmas, and a reduction to non-crossing pairs

The family is `sigma(o) = y`, `sigma(y) = o w_1 o`, `sigma(z) = o w_2 o`, with
`w_1, w_2` words over `{y, z}`; write `Y_i, Z_i` for their letter counts and
`L_i = |w_i|`.

*Lemma Φ0.* `det M = 2(Z_1 − Z_2)`. So a member has `|Z_1 − Z_2| = 1`, and
primitivity forces `Z_1 >= 1` (otherwise `z` occurs only in `sigma(z)`).

*Proof.* With rows and columns ordered `o, y, z`,
`M = [[0, 2, 2], [1, Y_1, Y_2], [0, Z_1, Z_2]]`; expand along the first
column. `square`

*Lemma Φ1.* If `w_1` begins with `y`, then `{o, y}` is coincident by level 3.

*Proof.* By Lemma W (§3d.1), with positions in the images. Level 1:
`sigma(o)[0] = y` against `sigma(y)[1] = y`, offset `−e_o`. Level 2:
`sigma(y)[2]` against `sigma(y)[1] = y`, offset `−e_y + pi(o y) − pi(o) = 0`.
`w_1 = y` alone is excluded by Lemma Φ0, so `sigma(y)[2] = w_1[1]`; if it is
`y` this is a shared tile at level 2, and if it is `z` the next level compares
`sigma(z)` and `sigma(y)`, which both begin with `o` after equal (empty)
prefixes. `square`

*Lemma Φ2 (crossing).* If `w_1` begins with `z` and there are `i, k >= 1` with
`pi(w_1[:i]) = pi(w_2[:k]) + e_y`, then `{o, y}` is coincident by level 3.

*Proof.* Level 1: `sigma(o)[0] = y` against `sigma(y)[1] = z`, offset `−e_o`.
Level 2: `M(−e_o) = −e_y`; take `sigma(y)[i] = w_1[i − 1] =: a` against
`sigma(z)[k] = w_2[k − 1] =: b`, offset
`−e_y + pi(w_1[:i − 1]) − pi(w_2[:k − 1]) = e_b − e_a` by hypothesis. If
`a = b` that is a shared tile. Otherwise take the final `o` of `sigma(a)` and
of `sigma(b)` (both `a, b ∈ {y, z}`): the offset is
`M(e_b − e_a) + (pi(sigma(a)) − e_o) − (pi(sigma(b)) − e_o) = 0`, a shared
`o`-tile at level 3. `square`

*When do the walks cross?* The hypothesis says that the lattice walk of
`w_1`'s prefix Parikh vectors meets the walk of `w_2`'s shifted by `e_y`;
lengths force `i = k + 1`. Put `T = min(L_1, L_2 + 1)` and
`g(t) = #_y(w_1[:t]) − #_y(w_2[:t − 1]) − 1` for `1 <= t <= T`. Then `g(1) = −1`
(as `w_1` begins with `z`), `g` moves by `−1, 0` or `1` per step, and the walks
cross exactly when `g(t) = 0` for some `t >= 2`. By the discrete intermediate
value theorem they cross whenever `g(T) >= 0`. **They fail to cross exactly
when `#_y(w_1[:t]) <= #_y(w_2[:t − 1])` for every `t <= T`**: `w_1`'s `y`-count
never catches up with `w_2`'s, one letter behind.

*Proposition Φ (reduction).* On Theorem K's family, `{o, y}` is eventually
coincident — so all-pairs strong coincidence holds — except possibly when
`w_1` begins with `z` and the pair is non-crossing in this sense. With
Theorem K: **on the whole catch-up-free `|det M| = 2` class, all-pairs strong
coincidence holds except possibly on the non-crossing members of this one
family.**

*Certificate and census* (`named_path`, `family_census` in
`kernel/odd_letter_family_certificate.mojo`, regression
`kernel/tests/test_odd_letter_family_certificate.mojo`). For every PIP member
with `|det M| = 2` and `L_1, L_2 <= 7` — **8,130 members** — the named path of
Lemma Φ1 (4,230) or Φ2 (2,633) verifies with `verify_witness` and names a
shared tile of the actual words, none failing; the **1,267 non-crossing**
members are decided by `coincidence_level`, all coincident, at levels 3
(1,110), 4 (150), 5 (6) and 6 (1). Between lengths 6 and 7 the level-5 and
level-6 rows move from 5 and 1 to 6 and 1. This is an exact finite check on
the stated domain, not a proof beyond it.

*What the non-crossing members use.* An exploratory look at their witnesses
(scratch only) finds them at levels 2 to 5, but through offsets such as
`k(e_z − e_y)` whose next step needs the *global* difference
`delta = pi(w_1) − pi(w_2) = (Y_1 − Y_2, Z_1 − Z_2)` to be realised as a
prefix difference — that is, they depend on how `delta` relates to the
local structure of the words, not on a crossing near the start. A proof for
them has to split on `delta`.

*A negative control on method.* The pattern tree of the same driver
(`cover_family`) refines the words letter by letter, with opaque tails and
relational splits, and closes nodes by parametric witness paths, Lemma P1
cuts and exact decisions. Its certified leaves are proofs on whole regions —
at depth 6 they hold 413 of the 532 members with `L_i <= 5`, each at a level
the exact procedure confirms — and no closed leaf holds a member it should
not. But it does not converge: its open leaves grow from 34 (depth 6) to 237,
969 and 4,083 (depths 9, 11, 13). Revealing letters is the wrong
decomposition for this family; the non-crossing analysis above says the
right one splits on `delta` and on the walk.

*What §3f establishes, and what it does not.* Lemmas Φ0–Φ2 and Proposition Φ
are proved here (unreviewed). The open part of the catch-up-free determinant-2
class is now the non-crossing members of one family, all of which are
coincident up to word length 7 by exact decision. Nothing beyond that is
claimed.

## 3g. The delta split: one cell closed, the rest measured

Split the non-crossing members of §3f by
`delta = pi(w_1) − pi(w_2) = (Y_1 − Y_2, Z_1 − Z_2) = (Delta, s)`, with
`s = ±1` by Lemma Φ0. `delta` matters because `M(e_z − e_y) =
pi(sigma(z)) − pi(sigma(y)) = −delta`: once `delta` is fixed, offsets along
`e_z − e_y` multiply to constants, and the witnesses of §3f's exploratory look
become uniform.

*Lemma Φ4 (Pisot signs).* If `s = +1` then `Delta >= 0`; if `s = −1` then
`Delta <= 0` and `Z_2 >= 2`.

*Proof.* Substituting `Z_1 = Z_2 + s` into `f(t) = det(tI − M)` gives the exact
identities `f(−1) = (1 + Z_2)(Y_2 − Y_1 − 1)` for `s = +1` and
`f(1) = (Z_2 − 1)(Y_1 − Y_2 − 1)` for `s = −1`. Lemma P1 needs both negative;
for `s = −1`, primitivity gives `Z_1 = Z_2 − 1 >= 1`. `square`

*Lemma Φ5 (the `s = +1` shape).* If `s = +1` and the pair is non-crossing,
then `w_1 = u y^Delta` with `pi(u) = pi(w_2) + e_z`.

*Proof.* `L_1 = Y_1 + Z_2 + 1 >= L_2 + 1`, so the non-crossing condition at
`t = L_2 + 1` reads `#_y(w_1[:L_2 + 1]) <= Y_2`. Then that prefix holds at least
`Z_2 + 1 = Z_1` letters `z`, hence all of them, and exactly `Y_2` letters `y`.
`square`

*Common points.* Call `t >= 1` a common point if
`pi(w_1[:t]) = pi(w_2[:t − 1]) + e_z` and both `w_1[t]` and `w_2[t − 1]` exist.
Since `w_1` begins with `z`, `t = 1` is one whenever `L_1 >= 2` and `L_2 >= 1`.
At a common point, the level-2 positions `sigma(y)[t + 1]` and `sigma(z)[t]`
give offset `−e_y + e_z` and letters `(a, b) = (w_1[t], w_2[t − 1])`. The pair
`(y, z)` would be a crossing, so in the non-crossing case `(a, b)` is `(y, y)`,
`(z, z)` or `(z, y)`.

Three lemmas close such states. Each is proved by Lemma W with the start trick
(offset 0 on two letters of `{y, z}`: both images begin with `o`) or the end
trick (offset `e_b − e_a`: both images end with `o`).

*Lemma Φ6 (matched step).* At a common point with `a = b`, if `w_a` (`w_1` for
`y`, `w_2` for `z`) has a factor with Parikh vector `delta` or `−delta` that is
not all of `w_a`, then `{o, y}` is coincident by level 4. *Proof.* The next
offset is `−delta` plus a prefix difference inside `sigma(a)`; a factor with
Parikh `±delta` cancels it, and a factor that is not both a prefix and a
suffix of `w_a` leaves a letter of `{y, z}` on one side for the start or the
end trick. `square`

*Lemma Φ7 (`delta = e_z`, unmatched start).* If `delta = e_z`, `w_1` begins
`zz` and `w_2` begins `y`, and `w_1` has a factor with Parikh `e_y + e_z`
other than all of `w_1`, then `{o, y}` is coincident by level 5. *Proof.*
Positions `sigma(y)[2]` against `sigma(z)[0]` give offset `e_o + e_z − e_y` on
`(z, o)`. Then `sigma(z)[1]` against `sigma(o)[0]` give
`e_y − delta + e_o = e_o + e_y − e_z` on `(w_2[0], y) = (y, y)`. `M` of that
is `e_y + delta = e_y + e_z`, which the factor cancels. `square`

*Lemma Φ8 (unmatched step).* At a common point with `(a, b) = (z, y)`, if the
walks of `w_2` and `w_1 + delta` share a point `pi(w_2[:m]) = pi(w_1[:n]) +
delta` with `m, n >= 1`, or with `n = 0` and `1 <= m < L_2`, then `{o, y}` is
coincident by level 4. *Proof.* The next offset is
`−delta + pi(w_2[:m]) − pi(w_1[:n])` at positions `sigma(z)[m + 1]`,
`sigma(y)[n + 1]` (start trick), or the same shifted back by one (end trick).
`square`

*Theorem Φ (the cell `delta = e_z`).* Every PIP member with `|det M| = 2`,
`Z_1 = Z_2 + 1` and `Y_1 = Y_2` has `{o, y}` eventually coincident.

*Proof.* Members with `w_1` beginning with `y` fall under Lemma Φ1, crossing
pairs under Lemma Φ2. For a non-crossing pair with `w_1 = z...`: if `w_2` is
empty then `w_1 = z`, whose matrix has `f(t) = t^3 − 2t − 2`, which is not
Pisot. Otherwise `t = 1` is a common point, and:

- `(y, y)`: Lemma Φ6, with the factor `z = w_1[0:1]` (`L_1 >= 2`);
- `(z, z)`: Lemma Φ6, with the factor `z = w_2[0:1]`. It fails only if
  `w_2 = z`, which forces `w_1 = zz`. That one substitution is decided by
  `coincidence_level` at level 6;
- `(z, y)`: Lemma Φ7. `w_2` contains a `y`, so `Y_1 = Y_2 >= 1`, and the first
  `y` of `w_1` follows a `z`, giving a factor `zy` of length `2 < L_1`.

`square`

*Certificate.* `phi_path` and `phi_census` in
`kernel/odd_letter_family_certificate.mojo` build each lemma's path from the
words and check it with `verify_witness` and against the words themselves.
`delta_census` checks Lemmas Φ4 and Φ5. The regression pins:

- Lemmas Φ4 and Φ5 on all 2,136 members with `L_i <= 6`;
- 1,180 lemma paths among the 1,267 non-crossing members with `L_i <= 7`,
  none failing, and 4,042 of 4,307 at `L_i <= 8` (driver run);
- `(zz, z)` at level 6, and `(z, ε)` not PIP.

*What remains, measured.* The other cells — `s = +1` with `Delta >= 1`, and
`s = −1` — keep a residue after Lemmas Φ6–Φ8: 87 members at `L_i <= 7` and 265
at `L_i <= 8`, all decided coincident. The residue is mostly at levels 3 and 4
(38 and 42 at length 7). The deeper rows do not move: 6 at level 5 and 1 at
level 6, at both lengths. The residue therefore grows, and these three lemmas
do not close those cells.

The residue has recognisable shapes, such as
`(z^a y^(Delta + b), y^b z^(a − 1))` with `Delta >= 1`. Offered to the
parametric witness-path search as cones, these close only with `Delta` fixed
— with `Delta` free, `delta` is not constant and no uniform path exists. Even
with `Delta = 1` fixed, only the part with both runs nonempty closes, at
level 6. So the next step in those cells is a split on `Delta` together with
relational splits of the run lengths, not more letters.

*What §3g establishes.* Lemmas Φ4–Φ8 and Theorem Φ (unreviewed): one infinite
cell of Theorem K's family is now closed. The open part of the catch-up-free
`|det M| = 2` class is the non-crossing members with
`delta ∈ {(Delta, 1) : Delta >= 1} ∪ {(Delta, −1) : Delta <= 0}`, minus those
Lemmas Φ6 and Φ8 cover.

## 3h. Delta and run-length splits: whole shape families closed

§3g left a residue in the cells `s = +1, Delta >= 1` and `s = −1, Delta <= −1`.
This section certifies whole infinite families inside those cells, organised
by **run shape**: the letters of the maximal runs of `w_1` and of `w_2`, with
the run lengths `>= 1` as variables. Every member has exactly one run shape.

*The cover of a shape cell.* `cover_shape` in
`kernel/odd_letter_family_certificate.mojo` takes a shape and one `delta`
cell — `s` together with an exact `Delta`, or a tail `|Delta| >= D` carried by
one extra variable. Each step below is a partition or a proof:

1. **Constraints as substitutions.** `Z_1 − Z_2 = s`, and an exact `Delta`,
   are linear equations with coefficients `±1` in the run variables.
   `solve_constraint` splits on `n_p >= n_q` against `n_q > n_p` (substituting
   `n_p = n_q + r` or `n_q = n_p + 1 + r`) until one side is empty, then
   enumerates. The regions partition the solutions;
   `test_the_constraint_substitutions_partition_the_solutions` checks every
   solution in a box is hit exactly once.
2. **Cuts.** Lemma Φ4's sign condition, and Lemma P1 in two further exact
   forms, verified as identities:

       s = +1:  f(1)  = Z_2 (Delta − 1) − 2 Y_2 − Delta − 3,
       s = −1:  f(−1) = Z_2 (|Delta| − 1) − 2 Y_1 − |Delta| + 3.

   A region where the quadratic is provably `>= 0` (all coefficients
   nonnegative over the orthant) has no PIP member.
3. **Witness paths.** First the constant-offset search of §3d. Then a new
   **line mode**: offsets may be *affine* along `e_z − e_y`, with their
   `o`-part and their `y + z` sum constant. Lemma W needs nothing of the
   offsets but bookkeeping; what keeps every check affine is that `M gamma`
   be affine, which holds because `M(e_z − e_y) = −delta` is constant in an
   exact-`Delta` cell. `m_times_affine` computes `M gamma` as an exact
   quadratic and refuses it unless the quadratic part vanishes.
   `verify_witness_line` re-derives every state. Line mode is needed: the
   cell `(zy | yz)`, `s = −1`, `Delta = −1` closes only with it. The witness
   there passes through the offset `(b + 1)(e_z − e_y)`, where `b` is a run
   length.
4. **Splits.** Relational splits `n_i > n_j | n_i = n_j | n_i < n_j`, then
   value splits `n = 0 | n >= 1`. Both are partitions.
5. **Points.** A region with no live variable is one substitution: screened
   exactly and decided by `coincidence_level`.
6. **Fail closed.** A region still open at the split limits, or beyond the
   per-cell budget of 600 regions, is reported open. Everything unexamined
   is counted open.

A cell with no open region is therefore a proof: every member with that
shape in that `delta` cell is coincident.

*Theorem Ψ (closed shape families).* With `Z_1 = Z_2 + 1`, every PIP member
with `|det M| = 2` is coincident on `{o, y}` when `(w_1, w_2)` has run shape
`(zy | ε)`, `(zy | z)` or `(zy | yz)`, for **every** `Delta`. That is the
families `(z^a y^b, ε)`, `(z^a y^b, z^c)` and `(z^a y^b, y^c z^d)` without
restriction on the run lengths. The other closed cells are fixed-`Delta`
cells, listed below.

*Proof.* `cover_shape` closes the cells `Delta = 1, 2, 3` and the tail

## 3i. Induction on runs: patterns with an open tail

§3h covers one run shape at a time, so each closed cell fixes the number of
runs. This section lets the number of runs be unbounded.

*Run patterns.* A **run pattern** gives the letters of the first runs of
`w_1` and of `w_2`, and says, for each word, whether it stops there or
continues with a **tail**: an opaque word known only by its Parikh vector
`(0, t_y, t_z)`, both free (`RunPattern`, `pattern_family` in
`kernel/odd_letter_family_certificate.mojo`). A pattern with a tail stands for
every word pair whose runs begin with the revealed ones — infinitely many run
counts at once. The witness search may cross a tail but never look inside it,
and every word with the tail's Parikh vector is admitted, so a closed pattern
is a statement about all of them.

*Refinement is a partition.* `refine_pattern` picks the open word with fewer
revealed runs (`w_1` on ties) and splits its tail: either it is empty, or it
is one more run of the letter other than the last revealed one (either letter
if none is revealed yet) followed by a new tail. Runs alternate, so every word
pair lies in exactly one child. `test_pattern_refinement_partitions_the_word_pairs`
refines the root four times and checks that each of the `31 · 63` pairs with
`w_1` beginning with `z` and both lengths at most 5 lies in exactly one leaf.

*The run tree.* `run_tree` starts from the root `z* | *` (`w_1` beginning with
`y` is Lemma Φ1; a `w_1` without `z` is not primitive), runs §3h's
`cover_pattern` on each pattern in one exact `delta` cell, and refines a
pattern that does not close. It stops refining at 6 revealed runs. Per
pattern it uses 3 relational splits, 3 value splits and 300 regions; past
those, the pattern is reported open (`RUN_TREE_*` in the driver). A leaf with
no open region is a theorem for every member of its pattern in its cell.

## 3j. The excursion route: a crossing lemma, two suffix lemmas, and what the cover still lacks

§3i ended on single excursions. This section follows the route proposed
there, records why its first form fails, and states what replaced it
(gate note: [`p1a-excursion-route-literature-gate-2026-10-06.md`](p1a-excursion-route-literature-gate-2026-10-06.md)).

*What the witnesses do.* The shortest witness of almost every residual
member starts `(o, y, 0) → (y, z, −e_o)`. Its level-2 state at a synchronized
time `t` is `(w_1[t], w_2[t − 1], c (e_z − e_y))` with `c = 1 − h(t)`. This
is the counter reading proposed in §3i. But the next step compares the walks
at a **lag**. Since `M(e_z − e_y) = −delta`, an interior step from
`(a, b, c (e_z − e_y))` reaches offset `−c delta + W_a(i) − W_b(k)`. That
offset is zero exactly when `W_a(i) = W_b(k) + c delta`, which forces
`i − k = c (Delta + s)`. No lockstep reading expresses that. The one-counter
route therefore stops. What it turned into is the following.

*Lemma X (monotone crossing).* Let `P: p_0 → p_1` and `Q: q_0 → q_1` be
lattice paths with unit steps `e_y`, `e_z`. Write `|v| = v_y + v_z`, and let
`n_lo = max(|p_0|, |q_0|)`, `n_hi = min(|p_1|, |q_1|)`, and suppose
`n_lo <= n_hi`. Suppose further that `Q` starts
weakly on one side of `P` (`q_0` weakly NW of `p_0`, or weakly SE), and ends
on the other side (`q_1` SE, resp. NW, of `p_1`), strictly. Then `P` and `Q`
share a point at a level `n` with `n_lo <= n < n_hi`. If the end condition
is only weak, they share one with `n <= n_hi`.

*Proof.* `g(n) = Q_y(n) − P_y(n)` is defined on `[n_lo, n_hi]` and moves by at
most 1 per level. The start condition gives `g(n_lo) >= 0`. The strict end
condition gives `g(n_hi) < 0`: if `n_hi = |q_1|`, monotonicity of `P` gives
`P_y(n_hi) >= n_hi − p_{1,z} > q_{1,y}`, and otherwise `Q_y(n_hi) <= q_{1,y}
< p_{1,y}`. The weak condition gives `<= 0`. Hence `g` vanishes on the stated
range. `square`

*Closure.* Take a state `(a, b, gamma)` with `a, b` in `{y, z}` and
`(M gamma)_o = 0`. Suppose `W_a` and `W_b − (M gamma)_{yz}` meet, by Lemma X,
at a point with a letter after it in both words. That letter exists when
the level is below `n_hi`, or when both ranges end inside their words. Then
the next offset is 0, with letters in `{y, z}`. Equal letters are a shared
tile. Unequal ones are one level from a shared tile, since `sigma(y)` and
`sigma(z)` both begin with `o`. Only the four endpoints are read, never the
arrangement between them. So the ranges may cross **opaque tails**, which is
exactly what §3i's patterns could not use. `monotone_paths_meet` and its
affine lift `crossing_holds`, `search_crossing` and `verify_crossing` in
`kernel/psc/cone_witness.mojo` implement this. Lemma Φ2 is its first instance.

*Lemma Φ5′ (the short suffix, `s = −1`).* Suppose `Z_2 = Z_1 + 1`, `w_1`
begins with `z` and the walks do not cross. Then `Delta <= 0`,
`|w_2| = |w_1| + 1 − Delta`, and the last `2 − Delta` letters of `w_2` hold
at least two `z`.

*Proof.* `Delta <= 0` is Lemma Φ4. Non-crossing at `t = |w_1|` gives
`#y(w_2[:|w_1| − 1]) >= Y_1`. So that prefix holds at most `Z_1 − 1` letters
`z`, and the rest of `w_2`, of length `|w_2| − |w_1| + 1 = 2 − Delta`, holds
at least `Z_2 − Z_1 + 1 = 2`. `square`

Together with Lemma Φ5 (`s = +1`: `w_1 = u y^Delta`,
`pi(u) = pi(w_2) + e_z`), this means: in each `Delta` cell, the longer word
is a prefix whose Parikh vector is tied to the shorter word, followed by an
explicit suffix of bounded length.

*How much a certificate must reveal* (`reveal_census`, driver mode
`reveal`). The certificate is an exact path through revealed positions,
followed by Lemma X on revealed points. Revealed means:
- the first `r` runs of each word;
- the suffix of Lemma Φ5 or Φ5′;
- the synchronized cuts (`w_1` near `|w_2| + 1`, `w_2` near `|w_1|`);
- for a closure, the lagged images of the other word's revealed points.

level ≤ 6, `r` capped at 8):

The information is there. At every one of 11,664 parameter points tested

**uniformization**: the certificate changes piecewise with the parameters,
and relational and value splits are blind to the inequalities it needs.

*Verdict on the route.*
- **Insufficient as first proposed:** the lockstep one-counter reading.
- **Sufficient in substance:** Lemma X with the suffix lemmas and the cut
  points. Through length 10, every non-crossing member has a certificate that reads a
  bounded part of its words, and the open regions of the parametric cover
  have certificates pointwise.
- **Not yet a proof.** The missing piece is a **certificate-guided
  partition**:
  - take a certificate at a base point of a region;
  - lift its positions to affine forms;
  - carve out the polyhedron where its inequalities hold;
  - recurse on the complement.

  Its known difficulty is that the inequalities have small non-unit
  coefficients, so the carving needs a lattice-point decomposition beyond
  `solve_constraint`.

## 3k. Certificate-guided partition: whole cells closed

§3j left the parametric cover one tool short: the certificates exist
pointwise but change piecewise, and blind splitting does not find the
pieces. This section supplies the tool and applies it.

*Exact integer decomposition.* A region is the image of an orthant under an
affine substitution of the cone variables. `impose_nonneg` splits a region
into finitely many regions that exactly cover the points where an affine form
`F` is nonnegative, for any integer coefficients. On each of them `F` is
nonnegative coefficientwise, so the ordinary verifiers accept the result.

Pick a variable `n_p` with coefficient `a > 0` and write `F = a n_p − R`. Then
there are two cases:
- `R <= 0`: `F >= 0` holds throughout.
- `R >= 1`: then `n_p >= ⌈R / a⌉`. Split the other variables by their residues
  mod `a` until `R / a` is affine, and substitute `n_p ← ⌈R / a⌉ + n_p`.

Both cases recurse on `R`, which omits `n_p`. With no positive coefficient,
the variables of `F` are bounded and are enumerated.
`test_impose_nonneg_partitions_exactly` checks every form
`a_0 + a_1 n_1 + a_2 n_2` with `a_0` in `−4..4` and `a_1, a_2` in `−3..3`.
On the box `[0, 5]^2`, each point with `F >= 0` is hit once and no other.

*Lifting and carving.* At a member point `ns` of a region, the point family
(the pattern at `ns`, tails still opaque) is searched: first for an exact
line-mode path, then for a Lemma X closure. `lift_path` replays the path on
the region. It keeps the same segments, and at each step takes the line
candidate whose offset agrees with the point's. It collects the forms that
the offsets and Lemma X need, instead of requiring them.
`cover_pattern_guided` then works as follows:
- `carve` partitions the region by those forms;
- every piece where all of them hold is re-verified by `verify_witness_line`
  or `verify_crossing`, and a failure raises;
- the complement pieces go back on the stack.

The base point is generic: distinct primes `2, 3, 5, 7, …`. Two worse
choices were tried first:
- From the origin, certificates exploit boundary values and carve slices like
  `n_0 = 0`, and the cover then peels one value at a time.
- With every variable 2, they exploit accidental equalities such as
  `2 n_0 = n_3 + n_6`. One such carve split into 8,307 regions inside and
  35,040 outside, against one inside and eight outside from the prime point.

`_carving_forms` drops forms that are already nonnegative, and duplicates.

*Superseded mechanism.* This section records the cover as first built, with
carving by exact decomposition. §3l.5 replaces the decomposition by regions
that carry their own inequalities, which partition by definition, and the
counts below are those of the first build; §3l.6 has the current ones.

*Results.* The guided partition closes the doubly open patterns that §3j's
blind cover could not:

| pattern | cell | blind cover (§3j) | guided partition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `zy* +y \| zy*` | `(+1, 1)` | open | 106 regions, closed |
| `zyz* +y \| yzy*` | `(+1, 1)` | open | 1,522 regions, closed |

## 3l. The proof of the closed cells, derived in full

This section derives Theorems Λ, Λ′ and Λ″ (below) from first principles in
one place. Every step is either a lemma proved here or earlier in this note,
or the statement that one deterministic, fail-closed computation finishes in
a stated way. The aim is that a reader can check the argument line by line,
and knows exactly what the computer contributes.

### 3l.1 The statement

Theorem K's family is `sigma(o) = y`, `sigma(y) = o w_1 o`,
`sigma(z) = o w_2 o` with `w_1, w_2` words over `{y, z}`. Write
`Y_i = #_y(w_i)`, `Z_i = #_z(w_i)`, `L_i = |w_i|`, `s = Z_1 − Z_2`,
`Delta = Y_1 − Y_2`. Ordering the letters `o, y, z`,
`M = [[0, 2, 2], [1, Y_1, Y_2], [0, Z_1, Z_2]]` and `det M = 2 s` (Lemma Φ0).
So `|det M| = 2` means `s = ±1`. A *member* is a choice of `(w_1, w_2)` for
which `M` is PIP (primitive, irreducible characteristic polynomial, Pisot
Perron root) and `s = ±1`. The *cell* `(s, Delta)` is the set of members with
those two values.

**Theorem (closed cells).** For each `(s, Delta)` among `(+1, 0)` (Theorem Φ),
`(+1, 1)` (Theorem Λ), `(−1, 0)` (Theorem Λ″) and `(−1, −1)` (Theorem Λ′),
every member of the cell is coincident on `{o, y}`. That is, for some `n`,
`sigma^n(o)` and `sigma^n(y)` share a tile: a position in each with the same
letter and equal Parikh vectors before it. Status: repository-proved,
unreviewed; the finite check of §3l.6 is an exact Mojo computation.

Theorem Φ (§3g) has a human-readable proof by Lemmas Φ6–Φ8. The other three
cells follow the proof below. It has five steps: the reduction to run
patterns (§3l.2), the meaning of a pattern (§3l.3), the four kinds of
certificate (§3l.4), the exact partitions (§3l.5), and the finite check
(§3l.6). §3l.7 lists what is trusted.

### 3l.2 Step 1: every member is under Φ1, Φ2, or one suffix root

Let `sigma` be a member of the cell `(s, Delta)`.

1. If `w_1` begins with `y`, Lemma Φ1 gives a shared tile by level 3.
2. Otherwise `w_1` begins with `z` (Lemma Φ0 forbids `w_1` empty). If there
   are `i, k >= 1` with `pi(w_1[:i]) = pi(w_2[:k]) + e_y`, Lemma Φ2 gives a
   shared tile by level 3. Say the member *crosses*.
3. Otherwise the member is *non-crossing*. Write
   `h(t) = #_y(w_1[:t]) − #_y(w_2[:t − 1])` for `1 <= t <= min(L_1, L_2 + 1)`.
   The lengths force `i = k + 1` in a crossing, so non-crossing says
   `h(t) != 1` for every `t >= 2` in range. Also `h(1) = 0`, because `w_1`
   begins with `z`, and `h` moves by at most 1 per step. So, by the discrete
   intermediate value argument, **`h(t) <= 0` throughout**.

Now the sign of `s` decides the shape. Lemma Φ4 (Pisot signs, from Lemma P1)
gives `Delta >= 0` when `s = +1`, and `Delta <= 0`, `Z_2 >= 2` when `s = −1`.

- *`s = +1`* (Lemma Φ5). `L_1 = L_2 + Delta + 1 >= L_2 + 1`. Then
  `h(L_2 + 1) <= 0` reads `#_y(w_1[:L_2 + 1]) <= Y_2`. So that prefix holds at
  least `Z_2 + 1 = Z_1` letters `z`, hence all of them, and exactly `Y_2`
  letters `y`. Hence `w_1 = u y^Delta` with `pi(u) = pi(w_2) + e_z`, and `u`
  begins with `z`. (If `L_2 = 0` the same holds, with `u = w_1[:1] = z`.)
- *`s = −1`* (Lemma Φ5′). `L_2 = L_1 + 1 − Delta >= L_1 + 1`. If `L_1 >= 2`,
  then `h(L_1) <= 0` reads `#_y(w_2[:L_1 − 1]) >= Y_1`. So that prefix holds
  at most `Z_1 − 1` letters `z`, and the remaining `2 − Delta` letters of
  `w_2` hold at least `Z_2 − Z_1 + 1 = 2`. If `L_1 = 1` then `w_1 = z`,
  `Z_2 = 2`, and the whole of `w_2`, of length `2 − Delta`, holds both `z`.
  Hence `w_2 = v x` with `|x| = 2 − Delta` and at least two `z` in `x`.

So every non-crossing member lies under a **suffix root**:
- `z* +y^Delta | *` for `s = +1`: `w_1`'s core `u` begins with `z`, `w_1` ends
  with `y^Delta`, and `w_2` is anything.
- `z* | * +x` for `s = −1`, one root for each word `x` of length `2 − Delta`
  with at least two `z`: for `Delta = 0` only `x = zz`; for `Delta = −1`,
  `x ∈ {zzz, yzz, zyz, zzy}`.

`suffix_roots` builds exactly these.

### 3l.3 Step 2: what a run pattern means

A run pattern (`RunPattern`) records, for each word:
- the letters of its first runs;
- whether it continues with an *opaque tail*;
- its explicit suffix.

The pattern stands for every pair whose cores (each word minus its suffix)
have exactly those first runs, followed by the tail when there is one.

*Refinement is a partition* (§3i). `refine_pattern` replaces an open pattern
by children. Either the tail of one word ends, or it continues with one more
run of the letter other than the last revealed one (either letter if none is
revealed yet), followed by a new tail. Runs alternate, so every pair under
the parent lies under exactly one child.
`test_pattern_refinement_partitions_the_word_pairs` checks this on all pairs
up to length 5. Hence the leaves of any finite refinement tree partition the
pairs under its root.

*The pattern family.* `pattern_family` turns a pattern and a region into a
cone family:
- each revealed run becomes a run of affine length `1 + n` (so `>= 1`);
- each tail becomes an *opaque* segment with free affine Parikh vector
  `(0, t_y, t_z)`;
- each suffix letter becomes a letter segment.

A member under the pattern is then an *instantiation*: the run lengths and
tail counts fix a point of the cone, and the tails' arrangement is one of
many with those counts. No certificate below ever names a position inside an
opaque segment. Lemma X reads only the endpoints of a range. So **a
certificate valid at a cone point is valid for every arrangement of the
tails at that point**. The family is a superset: it also contains tails whose
first letter repeats the last run's letter, which is harmless for a proof of
coincidence.

*The cell equations.* `_pattern_starts` imposes `Z_1 − Z_2 = s` and
`Y_1 − Y_2 = Delta` as linear equations, with coefficients `±1`, in the
pattern's variables (§3h, `solve_constraint`). It returns regions: affine
images of orthants, which together contain exactly the cone points satisfying
both equations, each exactly once
(`test_the_constraint_substitutions_partition_the_solutions`).

### 3l.4 Step 3: the four ways a region is settled

A region `R` is a pair: an affine substitution `S` of the pattern's
variables, and a list of *assumptions*, affine forms `A_1, …, A_r` in
`n ∈ Z_{>=0}^m`. It stands for the points `S(n)` with every `A_j(n) >= 0`.
A form `F` is *proved* `>= 0` on `R` (`Prover`) when, for some integers
`l_i, l_j >= 0`, `F − l_i A_i − l_j A_j` has every coefficient `>= 0`: then
`F(n)` is a sum of nonnegative terms at every point of `R`. The same test
reads a quadratic form, and `F = 0` is `F >= 0` and `−F >= 0`; with no
assumptions it is the plain coefficient-sign test. Each region the cover
examines is settled in one of the following ways, each proved here.

**(a) Cut: no member.** Lemma P1 says a PIP `M` has `f(1) < 0` and
`f(−1) < 0`, where `f(t) = det(tI − M)`. In this family these are exactly:
- `s = +1`: `f(−1) = (1 + Z_2)(Y_2 − Y_1 − 1)` and
  `f(1) = Z_2 (Delta − 1) − 2 Y_2 − Delta − 3`;
- `s = −1`: `f(1) = (Z_2 − 1)(Y_1 − Y_2 − 1)` and
  `f(−1) = Z_2 (|Delta| − 1) − 2 Y_1 − |Delta| + 3`.

These identities are pinned by `test_the_quadratic_pisot_identities`.
`pisot_cut_under` substitutes the region's affine counts into the sign
conditions of Lemma Φ4 and into the quadratic forms, and declares no member
only when the offending form is proved `>= 0` on `R`.

A second cut is McCormick's. Write the quadratic form as `f = a b + c`, with
`a = Z_2`, `b = |Delta| − 1` and `c` affine. For any integers `a_0, b_0`,
`f = G_3 + G_1 G_2` identically, where `G_1 = a − a_0`, `G_2 = b − b_0` and
`G_3 = a_0 b + b_0 a − a_0 b_0 + c`. So `f >= 0` where all three are `>= 0`.
`mccormick_cut` checks the identity coefficientwise and proves each `G_i >= 0`
on the region carved by them (below). At a non-member point `p` with
`f(p) >= 0`, the corner `(2, b(p))` gives `G_3(p) = f(p)`, so `p` lies in the
quadrant (`pisot_carve_forms`).

**(b) A witness path on the whole region** (Lemma W, §3d.1, cone and line
versions). A path names, at each level, a segment of each image and an affine
offset inside it. `verify_witness` (constant offsets) and
`verify_witness_line` (offsets affine along `e_z − e_y`) recompute every state
`gamma_(l+1) = M gamma_l + pi(sigma(a_l)[:i_l]) − pi(sigma(b_l)[:k_l])` from
the step data and check that:
- each offset `o` in a run of length `L` satisfies `o >= 0` and
  `L − 1 − o >= 0`, each proved on `R`;
- a letter segment has offset `0`, and no position falls in an opaque
  segment;
- `M gamma_l` is affine: `m_times_affine` expands it as a quadratic and
  rejects any nonzero quadratic part;
- the last state is `(a, a, 0)`, with each coordinate proved `= 0` on `R`.

Every condition then holds at every point of `R`, and Lemma W gives a shared
tile at each point.

**(c) A Lemma X closure on the whole region.** `verify_crossing` checks a
verified line-mode path to a state `(a, b, gamma)` with `a, b ∈ {y, z}`, and
then checks, on the whole region:
- the chosen segment ranges `[s_0, s_1)` of `sigma(a)` and `[t_0, t_1)` of
  `sigma(b)` hold no `o`, so the third coordinate of
  `M gamma + pi(sigma(a)[:i]) − pi(sigma(b)[:k])` is constant along them,
  and that constant is proved `= 0` on `R`;
- `sigma(y)` and `sigma(z)` begin with the same letter (`o`);
- the endpoint inequalities of Lemma X (§3j), each proved on `R`.

The strict form is used unless both ranges stop before a nonempty letter
segment in `{y, z}` (`length >= 1` checked). At each point of `R`, Lemma X
then gives positions `i`, `k` inside the ranges where the next offset is 0
and the letters lie in `{y, z}`. Equal letters are a shared tile. Unequal
ones give, one level later, the first letters of `sigma(y)` and `sigma(z)`,
both `o`, at offset `M · 0 = 0`. In both cases Lemma W applies.

**(b′, c′) The same over polynomials** (`psc/poly_line.mojo`). States,
offsets and conditions may be integer polynomials in the region's variables
(degree at most 8). `verify_witness_poly` and `verify_crossing_poly`
recompute every state exactly as (b) and (c) do, prove each offset condition
and each Lemma X inequality `>= 0` on `R` (the polynomial less multiples of
at most two assumptions has every coefficient `>= 0`), and prove the end
offset, or the third coordinate's offset, `= 0` on `R`. A polynomial `>= 0`
coefficientwise is `>= 0` at every point of the orthant, so each condition
holds at every point of `R`, and Lemma W (with Lemma X) applies there as
before.

**(d) A single point decided exactly.** A region with no live variable is
one substitution. It is either shown not to be a member (`|det| != 2`, or
`CubicScreen.is_pip` false), or decided by `coincidence_level`, the exact
decision procedure behind every census in this note. A negative answer would
raise "SC REFUTED"; none occurs.

### 3l.5 Step 4: the cover visits a partition

`cover_pattern_guided` keeps a stack of regions, initially the regions of the
cell equations (§3l.3), with no assumptions. It pops a region `R` and settles
it by (a), (b), (c) or (d), or shows it empty, or replaces it by regions that
partition it, in one of three ways.

- **Value peel** (`n = 0 | n >= 1`): the substitutions `n ← 0` and
  `n ← n + 1`, applied to `S` and to every assumption, cover each point of
  `R` exactly once.
- **Carving** (§3k). A certificate found at a point `ns` of `R` is lifted to
  `R` (`lift_path`, `crossing_conditions`). That yields affine forms
  `G_1, …, G_k`, all `>= 0` at `ns` (checked; a failure raises), under which
  the certificate's conditions hold. Forms already proved on `R` are dropped.
  The pieces are:
  - `R` with `G_1, …, G_k` added to its assumptions. There (b) or (c)
    re-verifies the certificate with the ordinary verifier, proving each
    condition from the assumptions, and a failure raises;
  - for each `j`, `R` with `G_1, …, G_(j−1) >= 0` and `−G_j − 1 >= 0` added,
    pushed back.

  A point of `R` lies in the first piece if every `G_j(n) >= 0`, and
  otherwise in the piece of the least `j` with `G_j(n) < 0`, that is
  `G_j(n) <= −1` since `G_j` is integer-valued. So the pieces partition `R`;
  no decomposition is needed. The McCormick cut carves the same way, by
  `G_1, G_2, G_3`.
- **The base-point peel**: a value peel, taken when no point is found.
- **The affine Lemma P1 split.** Where the quadratic form of (a) is affine
  on `R` (as when `Delta` is fixed), `R` is carved by it once: the piece
  with `f >= 0` holds no member, and the rest carries `f <= −1`.

*How lifts are found* (no step of the proof depends on it). Fix a point
path's segments. Then the letters of every state are fixed and the end
offset is linear in the run offsets, so asking for affine offsets with the
end offset `= 0` identically on `R`, each taking the point's value at `ns`,
is one linear system over Q (`solve_lift`, exact RREF; for Lemma X the
closing equation is the third coordinate's offset, `solve_crossing_lift`).
An integral solution is a path whose only conditions are its offsets'
affine bounds, by which `R` is carved. When the base point's path has no
solution, other point paths are tried (`enumerate_point_paths`). Whatever
is found is re-verified by (b′) or (c′) on the carved piece, and checked
once more in plain arithmetic at the base point; either failure raises.

*Pinning.* A variable whose propagated bounds coincide (`lo = hi`) has that
value at every integer point of `R` and is substituted, so a region whose
assumptions pin every occurring variable is one substitution and goes to
(d).

*Assumptions are kept exactly.* An added form `c + g L` (`g` the gcd of its
variable coefficients) is stored as `⌊c / g⌋ + L` (`tighten`): over the
integers `g L >= −c` iff `L >= ⌈−c / g⌉`, so the integer points are the same.

*Emptiness.* A region is set aside as empty, never settled as covered, only
when one of three sound tests says it has no integer point:
- a sum of at most three assumptions has no positive coefficient and a
  negative constant (it is `< 0` on the orthant);
- bound propagation crosses a pair of bounds (`propagate_bounds`): from
  `a_k n_k + R_k >= 0` and the largest value of `R_k` on the current box,
  `a_k n_k >= −max R_k`, so every bound holds at every point;
- an exhaustive search (`search_point`) branches each occurring variable over
  its whole (bounded) domain, propagating at every node, and finds nothing
  without truncation. Each branching partitions a domain.

- Fourier–Motzkin elimination of every variable from the assumptions and
  `n >= 0` (`fm_infeasible`): an empty rational relaxation has no integer
  point. Capped; a capped run claims nothing.

A piece of a carving shown empty is not pushed.

*Further exact partitions.* A live variable whose propagated domain has
fewer than four values is split by value. At the peel limit, before a
region is reported open: Fourier–Motzkin projects the assumptions on each
live variable (`fm_bounds`), and a region shown finite (at most 2,000
points) is decided point by point by (b), (c) or (d) on each point's own
family, a point past the exact procedure's bounds being reported open; and
an assumption whose strict version is infeasible holds with equality, so its
unit variable is substituted and the region reprocessed. Each is a partition
of the region's integer points.

*The x-block.* For `s = −1` the cell equations also impose Lemma Φ5′'s
`Z(x) >= 2` and `|x| = 2 − Delta` on the suffix block. These are imposed
exactly, as substitutions (`impose_nonneg`, then `impose_equal`), by the
following lemma.

*Lemma (exact imposition).* For a region `R` and an affine form `F`,
`impose_nonneg` returns regions that partition `{n ∈ R : F(n) >= 0}`, and on
each of them `F` has nonnegative coefficients.

*Proof.* By induction on the number of variables `F` depends on. Three cases:
- *Base.* If `F` already has nonnegative coefficients, the answer is `R`
  itself.
- *No positive coefficient.* If `F(0) < 0` the set is empty. Otherwise each
  variable of `F` is bounded by `F(0) / |a_k|`, and enumerating the values of
  one of them (`n_k ← v`) is a partition. Each piece recurses on a form with
  fewer variables.
- *Some coefficient `a = a_p > 0`.* Write `F = a n_p − Q`, where `Q` omits
  `n_p`. The sets `{Q <= 0}` and `{Q >= 1}` partition `R`, and the recursion
  imposes `−Q >= 0` and `Q − 1 >= 0`, each with fewer variables.
  - On `{Q <= 0}`: `F = a n_p + (−Q)` has nonnegative coefficients there.
  - On `{Q >= 1}`: the recursion leaves `Q` (composed) with nonnegative
    coefficients. Splitting each other variable whose coefficient `a` does
    not divide (`n_k ← a n_k + rho`, `rho ∈ [0, a)`) is a partition and makes
    `Q = a Q′ + c` with `Q′` affine. Then `F >= 0` iff
    `n_p >= Q′ + ⌈c / a⌉`, and the substitution `n_p ← Q′ + ⌈c / a⌉ + n_p` is
    a bijection from the orthant onto that set. It leaves
    `F = a n_p + (a ⌈c / a⌉ − c)`, with nonnegative coefficients.

`square` (`test_impose_nonneg_partitions_exactly` checks this on every form
`a_0 + a_1 n_1 + a_2 n_2` with `a_0` in `−4..4` and `a_1, a_2` in `−3..3`;
`impose_equal` substitutes through a unit coefficient and is checked the same
way.)

*Invariant.* Every member of the pattern within the cell lies in exactly one
region that is on the stack, has been settled, or has been shown empty.
Settled regions hold no member, or only members with a shared tile. So **if
the stack empties with nothing reported open and the budget not exhausted,
every member of the pattern in the cell is coincident.** If a region exceeds
the peel limit or the budget, it is reported open, never certified: fail
closed.

### 3l.7 What is trusted

The proof is computer-assisted in a precise sense:
- **Proved by hand:** the reduction (§3l.2), the meaning of patterns
  (§3l.3), each settling rule (§3l.4) and each partition (§3l.5).
- **Trusted:** the correctness of their Mojo implementations, and the run
  finishing as recorded.

Each implementation is pinned by a regression in
`kernel/tests/test_odd_letter_family_certificate.mojo`:
- the partition of refinement;
- `solve_constraint`, `impose_nonneg` and `impose_equal`, by brute force;
- the `Prover`, affine and quadratic, by brute force under every pair of
  small assumptions;
- `tighten`, bound propagation and the exhaustive point search, by brute
  force (the last on a system that propagation alone cannot refute);
- Fourier–Motzkin emptiness and projection, by brute force (on a wedge that
  propagation cannot refute);
- the McCormick quadrants, by brute force for both signs of `s`;
- the polynomial arithmetic and prover, the agreement of the polynomial and
  affine verifiers, and the linear lift (solved paths hold at every
  instantiated point of their carved region), in
  `kernel/tests/test_poly_line.mojo`;
- Lemma X, by brute force, with its strictness control;
- the crossing closure, against the exact level on every residual member at
  length ≤ 7;
- the Pisot identities;
- the line-mode path.

The verifiers re-derive every state from the step data alone and prove each
condition from the region's own assumptions. So a wrong *search* can only
fail to find a certificate, never certify a false one. A wrong *verifier*,
*prover*, *emptiness test* or *partition* could, which is why those carry
brute-force regressions. Nothing in the proof uses the base points except to
choose where to look, and case (d) for a single point.

## 3m. Bounded `Z_2`: every `Delta` at once

§§3g–3l close cells of fixed `Delta`. The tail cells (`|Delta|` unbounded)
do not close by refining run patterns with opaque tails
([`p1a-tail-literature-gate-2026-10-07.md`](p1a-tail-literature-gate-2026-10-07.md);
side-notes ledger, 2026-10-06/07). The obstruction is Lemma P1's boundary:
for `s = +1` it reads `(Z_2 − 1)(Delta − 1) − 2 Y_2 − 4 < 0`, quadratic only
through the product `Z_2 (Delta − 1)`. Fixing `Z_2` makes it affine, and
then the words of a member have finitely many run shapes, each symbolic in
`Delta` and in the run lengths.

**Theorem Ζ (bounded `Z_2`).** Every member of Theorem K's family with
`s = +1` and `Z_2 <= 2`, or with `s = −1` and `Z_2 <= 3`, is coincident on
`{o, y}`, for every `Delta`. Status: repository-proved, unreviewed; the
finite check is an exact Mojo computation.

*Proof.* Let `sigma` be such a member. If `w_1` begins with `y`, Lemma Φ1;
if it crosses, Lemma Φ2 (§3l.2). Otherwise:

- *`s = +1`.* Lemma Φ4 gives `Delta >= 0`. For `Delta = 0`, Theorem Φ.
  For `Delta >= 1`, Lemma Φ5 gives `w_1 = u y^Delta` with
  `pi(u) = pi(w_2) + e_z` and `u` beginning with `z`. So `u` has
  `Z_2 + 1 <= 3` letters `z`, hence at most 3 z-runs, and `w_2` at most 2.
  The pair `(u, w_2)` lies under exactly one fully revealed run pattern
  (runs alternate): `u` one of the `2 (Z_2 + 1)` alternating run words
  beginning with `z`, `w_2` the empty word or an alternating run word with
  at most `cap` z-runs; `w_1` closes with the run `y^(1 + e)`,
  `Delta = 1 + e`, `e >= 0` symbolic.
- *`s = −1`.* Lemma Φ4 gives `Delta <= 0`. Here crossing members need no
  separate treatment: `w_1` (beginning with `z`, at most `Z_1 = Z_2 − 1`
  z-runs) and `w_2` (at most `Z_2` z-runs) lie under exactly one fully
  revealed pattern, with `Delta = −e`, `e >= 0` symbolic.

Each pattern's region is split once by the value of Lemma P1's factor
`a = Z_2`, by exact substitution of a unit-coefficient variable (§3l.5,
`_split_by_factor`): the pieces `a = k`, `k` up to the cap, are covered, and
the piece `a > cap` is left out (it is outside the theorem). On a piece
`a = k` Lemma P1's form is affine and is split once (§3l.5). Every piece is
then covered by §§3l.4–3l.5, so by the invariant of §3l.5 every member of the
pattern with `Z_2 <= cap` is coincident. `zcap_cover` enumerates the patterns
and covers each; the finite check is that every pattern closes. `square`

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `s = +1`, `Z_2 <= 2` | `odd_letter_family_certificate.mojo 2 zcap 1 2 5000` | 60, all closed | 748 | 52 s |
| `s = −1`, `Z_2 <= 3` | `odd_letter_family_certificate.mojo 2 zcap -1 3 5000` | 56, all closed | 1,329 | 95 s |

`test_bounded_z2_closes_for_every_delta` pins `s = +1, Z_2 <= 1` (24

*What it does not give.* The theorem covers all `Delta` but not all
members: `Z_2` is unbounded, and each cap is a separate finite check. The
cost grows with the cap (`s = −1`: 2.5 s at cap 2, 95 s at cap 3; at cap 4
three patterns keep finite boxes of up to ~18,000 points open after every
finisher). A census of witness shapes (side-notes ledger, 2026-10-07) shows
the leading certificates at `Z_2 = 3` and `4` coincide, but the number of
distinct shapes grows with `Z_2` (20, 42, 86 at `Z_2 = 2, 3, 4`). A proof
for every `Z_2` needs either a reduction in `Z_2` or a uniform argument for
`Z_2` large; that is the open frontier of Theorem K's family.

*Where the uniform cover stops (measured, not a claim).* Lemma P1 enters
the cover linearly on `Z_2 >= floor` (`z_floor_forms`). Even so, neither
the open-tail run tree nor fully revealed patterns with more than a few runs

## 4. Proposition B: why A1′ is not a corollary of the import

*Proposition B.* The imported Barge–Diamond theorem cannot give A1′, and the
reason is a count.

*Argument.* Barge–Diamond (2002), Theorem 1, produces **one** eventually
coincident pair of distinct letters out of the `d(d-1)/2` available. Their
proof, read in full for
[`p1b-barge-diamond-configuration-gate-2026-10-02.md`](p1b-barge-diamond-configuration-gate-2026-10-02.md)
§3, has two cases: Case 1 (maximality) hands over two eventually coincident
segments *that start at the same point*, which is exactly an aligned
configuration and hence exactly A1′'s branch; Case 2 (determinism) closes by a
lattice contradiction that says nothing about which pair coincides. **Neither
case can be aimed at a prescribed pair.**

For `d = 2` there is only `2·1/2 = 1` pair, so the one pair the theorem
produces is the pair one wanted: Theorem 1 *is* strong coincidence, which is
why the two-letter case is settled. For `d = 3` there are three pairs, the
theorem names one, and the other two — which after the Barge–Diamond
eliminator are exactly the bad edges `{a,c}` and `{b,c}` — are left open. A1′
asks for the aimed version on one of them. `square`

So Theorem C does not bring A1′ within reach of the import; it identifies which
single aimed statement is needed.

## 5. Negative controls, and what remains

*Not by transitivity.* Eventual coincidence is stable under raising the level
(Theorem C step 4) but is **not** transitive: witnesses for `{i,j}` and
`{j,k}` at a common level decompose `sigma^n(j)` at two unrelated positions.
A proposed proof of A1′ that also gave transitivity would prove ternary strong
coincidence, so it is either wrong or a major result.

*Not by endpoint synchronization.* `p1a-aligned-cycle-normal-form-2026-10-01.md`
§5 already forbids replacing eventual coincidence by endpoint synchronization,
and Proposition A is the reason: in this configuration the endpoints never
synchronize, so the whole content is interior.

*Not by a uniform level bound, yet.* A1′ follows from a uniform bound on
residual coincidence level, by one finite check per level. That is a separate
open gate, `uniform-coincidence-level-bound-2026-09-18.md`, which proves the
per-substitution bound `|C_{sigma,i,j}| - 1` and records proved negative
controls against two naive uniformizations. The census's level data is the
evidence a uniform bound must respect: at most 15 over 408,798 specimens, with
no new deep pairs as the domain grows.

**What remains.** A1′ for every PIP `sigma` on three letters. By Corollary C3
the catch-up-free part of it is the same boundary-hitting statement as T2, so
the two open branches of productivity meet there; outside that class, Lemma A's
hypothesis `|det M| = 2` fails and the identification is not available.

## 6. What this does not establish

- **Not A1′, not #138, not strong coincidence.** Propositions A and B and

- **Not a new finite-domain result.** The 408,798-specimen figure is the

- **Proposition B is a reading of a source, not a theorem about `sigma`.** It
