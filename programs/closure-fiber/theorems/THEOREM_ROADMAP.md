# Theorem Roadmap

## T0 — Typing and normalization library

Establish the base-field and representation conventions used by every later theorem.

Deliverables:
- quadratic étale algebra representation (E=F[t]/(t^2-d));
- rank-two multiplication matrices;
- norm-one subgroup equations;
- exact split/nonsplit finite-field cardinality lemmas;
- no square-root or transcendental notation in theorem statements.

## T1 — Orthogonal image of Nielsen data

Given a finite group (G), a Nielsen tuple ((g_1,ldots,g_r)), and an orthogonal representation
[
rho:G\to O(V,B),
]
prove that
[
(rho(g_1),ldots,rho(g_r))
]
defines a finite-image point of the corresponding product-one representation variety.

Then characterize when the image lies in, or canonically projects to, a rank-one special orthogonal subgroup.

### Boundary result already established

If the represented image is abelian — in particular, if it lies in a rank-one torus — each Hurwitz generator becomes an adjacent transposition. Therefore the braid action factors through (S_r), and the pure braid group acts trivially.

**Consequence:** a torus-valued image alone cannot retain nonabelian braid information.

**Novelty threshold:** a useful T1 theorem must retain extra nonabelian structure, for example a torus normalizer, semidirect-product decoration, higher-dimensional orthogonal image, or a coupled finite-group/torus state.

## T2 — Hurwitz-closed trace-word state

Define
[
K_{ij}=operatorname{Tr}(rho(g_i)rho(g_j)^{-1}).
]

### Completed

- [x] prove symmetry for self-dual/orthogonal (rho);
- [x] derive the exact Hurwitz update law;
- [ ] determine whether pairwise (K) is closed in any targeted representation class; the general non-closure claim is not yet proved;
- [x] exhibit an exact (S_3) counterexample showing that determinant, rank, characteristic polynomial, and principal-minor multiset of (K) are not braid invariants.

See `T2_TRACE_GRAM_HURWITZ.md`.

### Revised target

Determine a finite trace-coordinate state closed under Hurwitz substitution for a restricted but nontrivial representation class, without claiming that pairwise (K) is universally non-closed.

Priority representation classes:

1. finite-field arithmetic in already-known SL2 trace-coordinate models; do not claim trace closure itself as new;
2. representations with image in a torus normalizer, to retain the smallest possible nonabelian decoration;
3. orthogonal targets where traces do not generate the full character algebra;
4. finite braid orbits where orbit-averaged trace-word invariants are computable exactly.

### Deliverables

1. define the trace-word algebra generated by all word traces reached by Hurwitz substitutions;
2. prove finite generation / finite closure in at least one useful representation class;
3. identify polynomial invariants of the closed state under:
   - simultaneous conjugation;
   - pure braid action;
   - full braid action under the relevant class-labeling convention;
4. preserve smallest counterexamples for every failed invariant.

## T3 — Split versus nonsplit finite-field comparison

For (F=mathbb F_q), construct nontrivial Hurwitz families carrying 2-dimensional quadratic representations with split or nonsplit connected rank-one image.

Do not re-prove fixed-dihedral orbit classification. Prove an arithmetic-family comparison theorem for one of:
- fiber cardinalities;
- distribution of torus-valued closure invariants;
- component counts;
- Frobenius traces after a controlled enrichment.

The theorem must use more than the elementary \(q\pm1\) count.

### T3 progress

- [x] reduce fixed-pattern pure Nielsen orbit sizes to \(J_2(q\pm1)/6\);
- [x] prove the mod-3 sign law: positive for \(q\equiv1\pmod3\), negative for \(q\equiv-1\pmod3\) once \(q\ge322\);
- [x] classify prime equality: exactly \(q=29,41,71\);
- [ ] resolve the remaining prime-power family \(q=3^k\), \(k\ge6\);
- [ ] determine whether a Frobenius-sensitive refinement yields stronger arithmetic geometry.

## T4 — Toric-to-unipotent degeneration

Construct an explicit group scheme
[
mathcal G	o S
]
with:
- generic fiber toric;
- special fiber with prescribed unipotent behavior;
- a multiplication map (mathcal G^r	omathcal G);
- a theorem describing specialization of the identity fiber.

No informal "parabolic limit" language is allowed without this construction.

## T5 — Projective compatibility theorem

Fix a projective absolute quadric and a rank-one quadratic torus. Construct an explicit comparison between:
- a Cayley–Klein cross-ratio coordinate;
- a character of the torus after passage to a splitting algebra.

A shared (mathbb G_m)-valued codomain is insufficient; the theorem must identify the maps.
