# T3 Arithmetic Benchmark — Four-Reflection Product-One Tuples

## Purpose

This note records an exact finite-family benchmark for the split/nonsplit torus-normalizer program. It is not claimed as a novel classification theorem; finite-dihedral Hurwitz orbit classification is prior art.

Let

D_{2n}=C_n \rtimes C_2,

with reflections r_x=(g^x,1), x in Z/nZ.

For a four-reflection tuple

(r_{x_1},r_{x_2},r_{x_3},r_{x_4}),

the product-one condition is

x_1-x_2+x_3-x_4 = 0 mod n.

Thus x_4 is determined by x_1,x_2,x_3.

## Lemma T3.1 — Generation criterion

The tuple generates the full dihedral group iff

gcd(n, x_2-x_1, x_3-x_1)=1.

### Proof sketch

The rotation subgroup generated by products of reflections is generated by exponent differences. Since x_4-x_1 is a Z-linear combination of x_2-x_1 and x_3-x_1 under the product-one relation, the full rotation subgroup is C_n exactly when those two differences generate Z/nZ.

## Proposition T3.2 — Raw generating product-one count

The number of ordered generating four-reflection product-one tuples in D_{2n} is

n J_2(n),

where

J_2(n)=n^2 prod_{p|n}(1-p^{-2})

is the second Jordan totient.

### Proof

Choose x_1 freely (n choices). Set a=x_2-x_1 and b=x_3-x_1. Then x_4 is forced by product-one, and generation is equivalent to gcd(n,a,b)=1. The number of primitive pairs (a,b) modulo n is J_2(n).

## Proposition T3.3 — Fixed reflection-class pattern for even n

Write n=2^e m with m odd. Fix the alternating class pattern

(even, odd, even, odd).

The number of ordered generating product-one tuples with this exact class-position pattern is

(n^3/8) prod_{p|m}(1-p^{-2}).

### Reason

x_1 has n/2 even choices. The relative differences satisfy a=x_2-x_1 odd and b=x_3-x_1 even. The 2-primary generation condition is automatic because a is odd. At every odd prime p|m, the pair (a,b) must not vanish simultaneously modulo p. Chinese remaindering yields the stated product.

Since there are six placements of two even-class and two odd-class reflections, the corresponding raw count over all such class-position patterns is six times this quantity.

## Proposition T3.4 — Local braid benchmark

For a reflection pair (r_x,r_y), let d=y-x.

Under the Hurwitz generator beta,

(x,y) -> (y,2y-x) = (x+d,y+d).

Hence

beta^k(x,y)=(x+kd,y+kd).

The beta-orbit length is

n/gcd(n,d),

and the pure beta^2-orbit length is

n/gcd(n,2d).

For a generating pair, gcd(n,d)=1. If n is even, d is odd and the pure orbit length is n/2.

## Split versus nonsplit finite-field benchmark

For odd q, the rational point groups of the two rank-one tori have orders

n_split=q-1,

n_nonsplit=q+1.

For the canonical generating product-one tuple

(r_0,r_1,r_0,r_{-1}),

the pure beta_1^2 orbit lengths are therefore

(q-1)/2 and (q+1)/2.

This difference is exact but elementary. It is a regression target, not the publication theorem.

## What T3 must exceed

A publishable T3 result should not merely restate:

- q-1 versus q+1;
- fixed-dihedral Hurwitz classification;
- the local orbit-length formula above;
- the Jordan-totient raw tuple count.

It should instead involve at least one additional arithmetic or geometric layer:

1. distribution of full braid-orbit sizes over Nielsen data as q varies;
2. Frobenius action on orbit strata;
3. dependence on the factorization patterns of q-1 and q+1;
4. a split/nonsplit comparison after imposing orthogonal conjugacy data;
5. asymptotic statistics of generating Nielsen classes across q.
