# Counterexample to the Arithmetic Bicycle Conjecture

Claim: ABC-ARITHMETIC-REALIZATION-COUNTEREXAMPLE.
Refuted claims: ABC-MAIN and ABC-REALIZATION-INVARIANCE.

## Statement

In the same ambient lattice $\mathbb Z^2$, with the same two column
labels, take

$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&5\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
A'=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\0&5\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{1}
$$

They are essential realizations of the same torsion-free arithmetic
matroid, but

$$
\operatorname{Bic}_5(A)=0,\qquad
\operatorname{Bic}_5(A')\cong\mathbb Z/5.
\tag{2}
$$

The same discrepancy holds at the mixed composite modulus 10:

$$
\operatorname{Bic}_{10}(A)=0,\qquad
\operatorname{Bic}_{10}(A')\cong\mathbb Z/5.
\tag{3}
$$

Consequently no isomorphism of their compatible modular bicycle towers
exists. The general arithmetic realization-invariance statement is false,
even at the level of module isomorphism classes.

## 1. All arithmetic data agree

There are exactly four labeled subsets. Their common data are:

| Subset X | r(X) | m(X) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Empty | 0 | 1 |
| First column | 1 | 1 |
| Second column | 1 | 1 |
| Both columns | 2 | 5 |

The first column is (1,0) in both matrices. The second columns (1,5)
and (2,5) are primitive because their coordinate gcds are one.
Each singleton quotient is therefore free of rank one, with trivial
torsion. Both full determinants are 5, so the full column subgroups
have index 5 in the ambient lattice. Their quotient groups are cyclic
of order 5. Empty-set quotients are $\mathbb Z^2$.

Thus both lists have all the same rank and multiplicity values, and
even the same quotient-group isomorphism class at every subset.
Their total rank is the ambient rank, so both realizations are essential.
They are torsion-free because m(empty)=1. They are **not surjective**,
since m(E)=5. Torsion-free does not mean that every subset quotient is
torsion-free; see the [definition audit](../sources/arithmetic-matroid-background.md).

The data also satisfy the arithmetic axioms directly: the underlying
matroid is the free two-element matroid, the divisibilities are immediate,
the molecule product identities are tautological, and the only
two-element alternating multiplicity sum is $5-1-1+1=4\ge0$.

## 2. Literal cuts and cycles modulo 5

For potential $(s,t)$, the cut images are

$$
A^\top(s,t)=(s,s+5t),\qquad
(A')^\top(s,t)=(s,2s+5t).
$$

Modulo 5 these are respectively (s,s) and (s,2s). Applying the matrices
to those vectors gives (2s,0) and (5s,0) modulo 5.

In the first case the cycle condition forces s=0, because 2 is invertible
modulo 5. In the second case every s is a cycle. Hence the second bicycle
module is exactly the line generated by (1,2), with five elements.
This proves (2), including the module isomorphism type, not only a
difference in embedded edge subsets.

## 3. Direct mixed-composite proof at modulus 10

Every cut for A has the form $(s,s+5t)$. Its cycle equations are

$$
2s+5t\equiv0,\qquad 5s+25t\equiv0\pmod{10}.
$$

The first equation implies t is even, so $5t\equiv0$.
It then implies $s\equiv0\pmod5$. The second equation, with t even,
implies s is even. Thus s is zero modulo 10 and the bicycle is zero.

Every cut for A' has the form $(s,2s+5t)$. Its cycle equations are

$$
5s+10t\equiv0,\qquad 10s+25t\equiv0\pmod{10}.
$$

They are equivalent to s and t both being even. Thus the bicycles are
precisely

$$
\{(2u,4u)\bmod10:\ u\in\mathbb Z/5\},
$$

the cyclic module generated by (2,4) of order 5. This proves (3) by
integer congruences over a composite ring, without a field-rank argument.

## 4. Why this refutes the stated conjecture

Realization invariance would require isomorphic bicycle modules at
every modulus, under the complete arithmetic data above. At modulus
5, and independently at modulus 10, the orders are one and five.
No group or module isomorphism is possible. In particular, no family
of isomorphisms compatible with modulus changes is possible.

An integral unimodular row change and a signed column permutation
always give invertible cut/cycle coordinate maps, whether or not the
matrix is TU. They cannot remove this order discrepancy. Therefore
the two realizations are inequivalent even if a relabeling is allowed.

For essential rank-one realizations, each singleton multiplicity fixes
the absolute value of its nonzero entry, and the rank detects zero
entries. Equal arithmetic data therefore give column sign equivalence
and isomorphic bicycle towers for all moduli. Rank zero is trivial.
The example has the smallest possible rank and number of elements
among essential free-lattice realizations; no minimal ambient index
claim is made.

## 5. Scope and misleading proxies

Both Gram determinants are 25:

$$
AA^\top=\begin{pmatrix}2&5\\5&25\end{pmatrix},\qquad
A'(A')^\top=\begin{pmatrix}5&10\\10&25\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Their Smith factors are (1,25) and (5,5). These Gram groups are not
used to prove (2)–(3): the matrices have column index 5 and no integral
right inverse, so the fixed-TU forward formula cannot be assumed.

At modulus 25 both bicycle groups have order five. Agreement at that
prime power does not repair their discrepancy at 5 or 10.

The graph, fixed-TU, and regular TU representation-independence theorems
remain proved. Their hypotheses exclude the pair in (1).
The earlier A=[2] torsion-group-model obstruction is a separate negative
result and remains valid. Here there are two realizations with exactly
the same arithmetic data, so the principal realization-invariance
conjecture itself is refuted.

A surjectivity restriction or richer realization data would be a
different statement requiring its own source/proof audit. Neither
is silently substituted for the withdrawn conjecture.

## 6. Independent evidence and authority

The four arithmetic records are computed by certified Euclidean Smith
reduction and independently by gcds of all minors. Candidate bicycle
enumeration uses normalized potentials and deduplicates their gradients.
The independent oracle enumerates edge vectors and checks modular cut
membership by lattice indices of $[A^\top\mid nI]$ and its augmentation.
A third literal cut enumeration cross-checks the finite results.

The versioned fixture contains both complete arithmetic signatures,
prime/prime-power/mixed-composite profiles, and direct expected sets
at 5 and 10. The exact proof in Sections 1–4 is the refutation authority;
finite computation and source-pinned receipts support its implementation.
This is a repository-local explicit counterexample, with no novelty claim.
