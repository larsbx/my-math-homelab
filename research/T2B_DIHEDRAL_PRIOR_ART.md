# T2B Prior-Art Boundary — Dihedral Hurwitz Orbits

## Identification

For a finite cyclic torus-point group T(F) ≅ C_n, the rational-point normalizer model N(T)(F) ≅ C_n ⋊ C_2 with inversion action is a dihedral/generalized-dihedral group.

Therefore the Hurwitz dynamics derived in the torus-normalizer note sit inside an established literature on Hurwitz equivalence in dihedral groups.

## Prior art

Charmaine Sia classified Hurwitz orbits for tuples in dihedral, dicyclic, semidihedral, and related groups.

Emily Berger subsequently gave a complete classification for tuples in finite dihedral groups. Her invariants include:

- the product of the tuple entries;
- the subgroup generated by the entries;
- the number of entries in each conjugacy class of the generated subgroup.

Accordingly, classification of braid orbits in a single finite dihedral group is not a novelty target for this repository.

## Benchmark family

Let D_{2n}=C_n ⋊ C_2 with C_n=<g>, and write reflections r_x=(g^x,1), x in Z/nZ.

For two reflections, beta(r_x,r_y)=(r_y,r_{2y-x}).

Writing d=y-x, iteration gives beta^k(x,y)=(x+kd,y+kd).

Hence the orbit under the cyclic subgroup generated by beta has length n/gcd(n,d).

The pure generator beta^2 has orbit length n/gcd(n,2d).

These formulas are elementary consequences of the normalizer coordinates and should be treated as a regression benchmark, not as a new classification theorem.

## Four-reflection product-one benchmark

Consider r_n=(r_0,r_1,r_0,r_{-1}).

Its product is the identity because the exponent condition for four reflections is x_1-x_2+x_3-x_4=0.

It generates the full dihedral group because the reflection-exponent differences generate Z/nZ.

For even n, the pure braid generator beta_1^2 has orbit length n/2.

For odd q:

- split torus normalizer: n=q-1, giving orbit length (q-1)/2;
- nonsplit norm-one torus normalizer: n=q+1, giving orbit length (q+1)/2.

The difference is exactly one.

## Research consequence

The preceding split/nonsplit orbit-length comparison is useful as an exact sanity check, but is too elementary and too close to the known dihedral classification to serve as the project's main theorem.

A viable T3 theorem must instead compare families as q varies and use arithmetic structure not exhausted by the classification of one dihedral group. Candidate directions include:

1. distribution of braid-orbit lengths across Nielsen classes as q varies;
2. dependence on the factorization of q-1 versus q+1;
3. Frobenius/Galois action on orbit strata;
4. comparison after imposing geometric conjugacy-class data inherited from split versus nonsplit orthogonal groups;
5. statistics of generating tuples rather than arbitrary tuples.
